Robots are often chosen as if they will work in a perfect world, where parts lie neatly, cartons are lined up and the pallet was built by a storekeeper who is never late. In that world a robot needs no eyes. It learns a set of points and repeats them until retirement.

In the real world, parts are tipped into a bin, cartons on a pallet vary in size and are slightly crushed, and the storekeeper is late. Here a blind robot becomes a very expensive, very confident machine that picks up thin air.

At Aura Robotics we source robots and tooling directly from Chinese factories. We have written about grippers and cameras in general. This is a profile of a company that turned 3D vision into a business of its own, and an argument for treating vision as a separate purchase.

### Who they are

Mech-Mind Robotics was founded in 2016 by Shao Tianlan. It does not make robots. It makes what goes on or above them: Mech-Eye industrial 3D cameras, software and, more recently, the five-fingered Mech-Hand. It calls the concept “eye, brain, hand”.

On 1 September 2026 Mech-Mind listed in Hong Kong under the code 09615, at HK$101.70 a share, raising about HK$2.35 billion at a valuation of roughly HK$12.7 billion. It closed its first day 1.9% below the offer price. Cornerstone investors included Baillie Gifford, Jane Street and a BYD subsidiary.

A down first day is information too. The market is saying: we believe you, just not quite that much.

### The numbers

Revenue was 180.8 million yuan in 2023, 268.8 million in 2024 and 388.8 million in 2025, with 106.9 million in Q1 2026, up 73%. Gross margin rose from 39% to 65%, very high for a hardware maker and a sign that much of the value sits in software.

It is still loss-making, with an adjusted (non-IFRS) loss of about 109 million yuan in 2025.

According to CIC data in the prospectus, Mech-Mind ranks first globally in AI and 3D vision-guided robot components, with 22.1% of 2025 revenue, and first by shipments. Its systems are used in almost 50 countries, with more than 29,000 sets deployed. In 2025 overseas revenue passed half the total for the first time. Customers include more than 100 Fortune Global 500 companies, among them CATL, BYD, Midea and Foxconn.

### The camera range

Choose by working distance and task, not by price or how nice the housing looks.

- **UHP**: around 300 mm, micron-level accuracy, for inspection and measurement.
- **NANO** and **NANO ULTRA**: compact, 250–800 mm, for precise location, assembly and welding in tight spaces.
- **PRO S** and **PRO M**: 500–2,000 mm, general robotics at short and medium range.
- **ULTRA M**: 1,000–2,200 mm, demanding work including thin and transparent parts.
- **LSR S, L, XL**: 500–3,500 mm, laser-based, including use under ambient light and outdoors; large fields of view, de[palletizing](/en/catalog/palletizing), big parts.
- **DEEP**: 1,200–3,500 mm with a deep depth of field, for tall pallets and deep bins.

The software comes alongside: **Mech-Vision** (no-code image processing and recognition), **Mech-Viz** (robot path planning) and **Mech-DLK** (training neural networks on your own parts). It works with robots from many manufacturers, not just one brand.

A camera without software is a photo booth. Software without a camera is wishful thinking. They only sell as a pair.

### What it is for

**Bin picking**: parts lie in a bin at random, and the robot must find the top one, work out its orientation and plan a grasp without hitting the bin walls. This is the job 3D vision was invented for.

**Mixed-case depalletizing**: cartons of different sizes, sometimes wrapped, labelled or dented. A wide-field camera works out where one box ends and the next begins. Simple, until you see two identical boxes pushed together turn into one big box for an ordinary camera.

**Seam tracking**: the camera finds where the seam actually is, not where the program thinks it is. In short-run production with imperfectly tacked parts, that is the difference between a good weld and scrap.

**Machine tending, assembly, inspection**: locating parts before placement, checking presence and dimensions.

### Vision as a separate purchase

This is the real point of the article. Many buyers see the camera as a robot option, a checkbox. That is a mistake.

First, for bin picking and mixed depalletizing, vision and software often decide whether the cell works at all. A dozen robots might suit the job; the camera and software choice is much narrower.

Second, vision is a project: matching camera to working distance and field of view, lighting, calibration, training on your parts, testing on defects, glare, dark and shiny surfaces. A camera chosen from a catalogue rather than tested on real parts is a lottery ticket.

Third, vision often outlives the robot. With brand-agnostic software, camera and licence can move to another cell or another arm.

So for bin picking and mixed cartons, we recommend starting with a test: real parts, real lighting, recorded results. Then choose the robot to suit what the camera reliably sees.

First teach the robot to see. Teaching it to hold things can wait.

### What it means for buyers

Mech-Mind has high margins, growth and post-IPO cash, but no profit yet. That gives reasonable confidence in continued support and updates, but the usual questions still apply: warranty, spare cameras, software licences (perpetual or subscription), offline operation, interface language.

We select robots and vision systems for the task, calculate payback for the whole cell rather than just the hardware, buy directly from manufacturers, show the specific unit with its serial number on video from the factory floor and test it on the task before shipment. One team runs the project from selection to start-up. Aura Robotics Tour trips let you see 3D-vision cells working in Chinese plants.

### The bottom line

A robot without vision is like a very strong person in a blindfold. It will lift whatever it is handed. The trouble is what it gets handed.

Mech-Mind decided it pays better to sell sight than strength. Judging by its margins, it was right. The robot does not mind either way. It can finally see what it is picking up.
