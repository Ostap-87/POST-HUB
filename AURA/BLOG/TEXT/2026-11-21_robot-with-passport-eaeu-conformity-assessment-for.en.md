A robot may lift 20 kg, climb stairs and recognise faces. But it enters the Eurasian Economic Union the way any machine tool does: with paperwork. Without it, the robot waits in a bonded warehouse, mastering the key skill of imported equipment — patience.

At Aura Robotics we bring robots from Chinese factories and go through it regularly. Here is what to know before the robot boards the ship. One caveat up front: this is the general procedure. The exact set of documents for a specific model is agreed with a certification body, and that conversation is best started before payment, not after arrival.

### 1. The three regulations you meet most often

In the EAEU, product requirements are set by Customs Union and EAEU technical regulations (TR CU / TR EAEU). For robots, three come up most often.

**TR CU 010/2011, On the Safety of Machinery and Equipment.** The core regulation for industrial equipment.

**TR CU 004/2011, On the Safety of Low-Voltage Equipment.** Covers electrical equipment rated 50–1,000 V AC and 75–1,500 V DC. A curious detail: many mobile robots and humanoids run on batteries below 75 V. Whether the regulation applies to the robot itself, to its mains-powered charger, or to both is decided product by product.

**TR CU 020/2011, Electromagnetic Compatibility.** It applies to almost any electronics.

Which apply depends on the product: an arm in a cell, a [cobot](/en/catalog/cobots), a mobile robot and a humanoid can fall under different combinations.

### 2. What changed in 2026

This matters most for purchases being planned now.

Decision No. 36 of the Eurasian Economic Commission Council, dated 13 March 2026, substantially amends TR CU 010/2011. The decision took effect on 1 September 2026, but the new Annex 3 — the list of products subject to mandatory conformity assessment — applies from 1 April 2028.

For the first time, the new list names “robots and robotic devices” explicitly, placing them among products whose conformity is confirmed by declaration. The current list has no separate entry for robots, so today the classification of a specific robot is something to settle with the certification body.

Warehouse operators should note another line: “floor-based trackless industrial transport” goes into the certification list in the new edition. Whether that covers a particular warehouse mobile robot is worth checking with a certification body early.

Transition rules: conformity documents issued or adopted before the decision took effect remain valid until they expire, but no later than 1 September 2031.

A regulation is like a satnav: sometimes it recalculates the route. Better to find out before the turn.

### 3. Declaration or certificate

These are two forms of conformity assessment.

**Declaration.** The applicant declares that the product complies, based on evidence: test reports from an accredited laboratory and technical documentation. The declaration is entered in a unified register. Responsibility rests with the applicant.

**Certificate.** Issued by an accredited certification body after testing and, depending on the scheme, an assessment of production. It takes longer; responsibility is shared.

Either can cover serial production or a single batch: a batch suits a one-off purchase, serial production suits regular deliveries of one model.

Valid declarations and certificates can be checked in the public registers of Rosaccreditation, Russia’s accreditation service. Do that with any document a supplier shows you. A document that is not in the register is not a document. It is nice paper.

### 4. Who the applicant is

The applicant must be a legal entity or sole trader registered in an EAEU member state: the manufacturer, its authorised representative, or the importer. A Chinese factory cannot apply on its own. Someone inside the Union has to take on that role and responsibility.

If a document was issued to a different importer, you generally cannot import under it.

### 5. Which documents to prepare

The package usually includes: a technical description and product passport; an operating manual in Russian — not machine-translated, but something an operator can actually work from; for machinery, a safety justification, i.e. a risk analysis with mitigation measures; a list of applied standards; test reports; photos and marking.

Chinese factories often have much of this already, such as technical files for the European market. But “already have” does not mean “fits”: documents need translating and checking against EAEU requirements.

The EAC mark goes on the product and its documentation after conformity has been confirmed. Not before.

### 6. If the robot uses encryption

A modern robot almost always communicates: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cloud control, secure channels. If it includes encryption functions on the list set by the Eurasian Economic Commission, importing it into Russia may require a notification on the characteristics of the encryption, registered with the FSB and entered into a unified register. The manufacturer or its authorised representative files it. According to consultants, registration takes at least three to four weeks from submission, not counting preparation and the manufacturer’s power of attorney.

Whether a specific robot needs one depends on what is inside it. Ask the factory early.

### 7. How long it takes

The honest answer: from a few weeks to a few months, and almost always longer because of paperwork rather than testing.

Time goes on waiting for documents from the factory, translating the manual, delivering a test sample, and a notification someone remembered only when the robot was already in port.

Conformity assessment takes exactly as long as the documents took to arrive.

So the right order is: choose the model, immediately request the technical file and wireless module details from the factory, show them to a certification body, get the list of requirements, and build the timeline into the delivery schedule. Then the papers are ready when the robot reaches the border, not a month later.

A robot does not care which document it has. It will work the same with a declaration, with a certificate and, unfortunately, without either.
