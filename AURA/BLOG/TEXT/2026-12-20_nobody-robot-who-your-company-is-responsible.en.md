When the first robot arrives, the whole company comes to look at it. The chief engineer looks at the arm, the CFO looks at the invoice, HR looks at the chief engineer. A month later the robot is working and nobody is standing around it. Which is perfect, right up until the day it stops.

On that day a six-digit error code appears on the pendant, and everyone discovers that the robot is on the balance sheet but in nobody’s job description. It is an asset with no owner. A well-funded orphan.

At Aura Robotics we source robots directly from Chinese factories, and one team stays with the client from selection to launch. In almost every project someone eventually asks: “So who is going to look after it?” The best time to ask is before the purchase order. Here is how we break it down.

### 1. Three roles, not one job title

“Roboticist” sounds impressive and means very little on an org chart. In practice there are three roles around a robot, and they are not always three people.

**Operator.** Starts and stops the robot, loads parts, selects programs, reads error messages, stops safely and knows whom to call. Usually a former machine operator or warehouse worker with extra training. The operator does not need to program. The operator needs not to be afraid.

**Technician (cell programmer).** Adjusts paths and points, adds new parts, recovers after emergency stops, performs routine maintenance and keeps program backups. This is the person who decides how long downtime lasts.

**IT and security owner.** Network, accounts, firmware updates, remote access for the manufacturer, backups. Often not a separate position, just a line in someone’s existing job description. The point is that the line should exist in writing, not in folklore.

Above them sits a manager accountable for the robot’s numbers: output, downtime, reasons. Without one, a year later the robot looks like a good investment that nobody can prove.

An unowned robot does not break more often. It just stays broken longer.

### 2. How many people for one, five and twenty robots

There is no universal ratio; the figures below are illustrative planning assumptions, not a standard.

**One robot.** No new position needed. Train at least two operators for every shift the robot runs, plus one or two people with technician skills. Why two? Because knowledge held by one person is not a capability. It is a hostage of the holiday rota.

**Five robots.** The technician role becomes a main job for one person, better two if you run two shifts. IT can still sit with an existing employee, but write it down: update policy, access list, backup schedule.

**Twenty robots.** Now it is a small department: a senior robotics engineer, shift technicians, a dedicated network and data owner, a failure and spare-parts log. Fleet uniformity matters here. Twenty robots of one model are far easier than twenty robots of eight models. Every new model means new training, new spares and a new password someone will forget.

An illustrative example: five [cobots](/en/catalog/cobots), two shifts. Two technicians, one per shift, plus two trained operators per shift drawn from existing staff. Two new hires, not five. (Illustrative only; your shifts and holidays will change the numbers.)

### 3. Occupational standards: what already exists on paper

Russia does not yet have one obvious standard titled “industrial robot maintenance specialist”, but several neighbouring ones work well as a base for job descriptions.

**“Mechatronics worker in automotive manufacturing”** (code 31.002, Ministry of Labour order No. 826n of 22 November 2023). Despite the industry name, it describes exactly what a robot technician does: installation, adjustment, maintenance and repair of mechatronic systems, drive tuning, diagnostics of robotic cells. It replaced a 2018 version.

**“Mobile robotics operator”** (order No. 84n of 3 March 2016): sensor setup, operating a mobile robotic system, keeping it running, handling emergencies, preparing control programs. A useful template for owners of AMRs, robot dogs and inspection robots.

**Instrumentation and automation technician** (40.158) and **instrumentation fitter** (40.067) cover the sensors, cabinets and automation around the robot.

A standard will not teach anyone to fix a robot. It will save HR from inventing a job title no candidate understands.

### 4. Where to train people

**Vocational colleges.** The programme 15.02.10 “Mechatronics and Robotics” follows a federal standard approved in September 2023 (order No. 684). After nine school years it takes 3 years and 10 months. A trainee from such a college is an inexpensive way to grow a technician for your own fleet.

**The manufacturer.** Basic training usually comes with commissioning. Agree in advance on language, scope and who receives the documentation. If training goes through an interpreter, record it. Six months later nobody will remember what the engineer meant by “mandatory”.

**Your own programme.** Large operators have long grown technicians in-house. Amazon’s Mechatronics and Robotics Apprenticeship, announced in 2021, starts with 12 weeks of classroom training and a year on the job, followed by about two more years for top performers. Amazon says pay can rise by roughly 40% after the first stage and about 48% more after the second. Your scale is different, but the principle travels: your best future robot technician may already be working in your warehouse.

### 5. Why this will get harder

According to Rossiyskaya Gazeta (June 2026), Russian manufacturing had 40 robots per 10,000 workers in 2025, up 36% in a year. The national target is 145 by 2030, with the installed base growing from about 21,000 to more than 100,000 units. Experts quoted in the same piece name system integrators as a bottleneck.

Translated from statistics: robots will multiply faster than the people who understand them. You will have to grow your own.

### The bottom line

A robot never attends meetings, never files requests and never asks for a day off. That is why it gets left out of the org chart. It will remind you it exists, usually on the night shift, with an error code nobody is there to read.

Every machine needs someone who knows it by name. Or at least by serial number.
