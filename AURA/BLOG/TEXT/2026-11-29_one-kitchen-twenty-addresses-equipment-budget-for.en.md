A chain with five cafés has five kitchens and five head cooks, each of whom knows the right way to do it. At twenty cafés there are twenty right ways. A central kitchen is not there to cook more. It is there to make “the right way” singular again.

At Aura Robotics we source food production and central kitchen equipment directly from manufacturers in China. Below is a model project: a chain of 20 outlets, one production site, illustrative but honestly calculated numbers. It is not a budget for your chain. It is a way to see what your budget will be made of.

### 1. Model assumptions

Twenty cafés, each selling about 200 dishes a day: 4,000 portions in total. An illustrative menu split:

- 40% hot dishes from combi ovens: meat, poultry, fish, bakes;
- 30% soups, sides and sauces from kettles;
- 30% cold dishes: salads, starters, desserts.

The method is cook-chill: cook, chill fast, pack, deliver, reheat at the outlet. We cover chilling regimes in our article on cook-chill and blast freezing, and packaging in our article on tray sealers and modified atmosphere. Here we put it all under one roof.

A central kitchen is not a big kitchen. It is a small factory that happens to smell of soup.

### 2. Flow: raw never meets ready

A central kitchen is laid out around the flow, not the walls. Product moves one way, dirty to clean and raw to cooked, with no doubling back or crossing. This is not a design whim: the Customs Union food safety regulation TR CU 021/2011 requires manufacturers to set the sequence and flow of operations so as to prevent contamination.

Zones along the flow:

1.  **Goods-in** with scales and incoming [inspection](/en/catalog/inspection).
2.  **Stores:** dry, chilled at 0–4 °C, frozen at −18 °C.
3.  **Preparation:** vegetables (washing, peeling, cutting) and meat and fish (butchery, mince, semi-finished products).
4.  **Hot kitchen:** combi ovens, kettles, tilting pans.
5.  **Blast chilling**, right after the hot kitchen, not “wherever there was space”.
6.  **Cold kitchen and assembly:** salads and portioning in a clean zone.
7.  **Packing and labelling.**
8.  **Dispatch:** finished goods cold room and route loading.
9.  **Washing** for crates, pans and utensils, with separate dirty and clean flows.

The design is checked against current sanitary rules; for Russian catering, that has meant SanPiN 2.3/2.4.4282-26 since 1 September 2026. If product is sold beyond your own chain as packaged food, food production regulations apply on top. Find out where that line falls for your business model before design starts.

### 3. Hot kitchen: size by load, not by portions

**Combi ovens.** Hot dishes: 1,600 portions. At 150 g of main component that is 240 kg of cooked product. Illustratively, a 20-level GN 1/1 combi at about 4 kg per level takes 80 kg; a cycle including loading and unloading takes an hour and a half. An eight-hour shift gives five loads, about 400 kg. One oven would do. But one oven is a single point of failure for the whole chain. So the model has two: a 20-level and a 10-level. The second is not a luxury, it is an insurance policy that also works for a living.

**Kettles.** Soups: 1,200 portions at 250 ml, or 300 litres, plus sides and sauces. The model has two 250-litre tilting kettles with agitators and a tilting braising pan. Kettles are never filled to the brim, so size them by working volume and batches per shift.

**Cold kitchen:** vegetable cutters, a bowl cutter, a mixer, refrigerated prep tables. **Prep rooms:** a mincer, a mince mixer, forming if needed.

### 4. Cold: blast chilling and storage

Total hot output per shift, including soups and sides, is about 900 kg. By the guidance we discussed, each batch should reach +3 °C within two and a half hours of cooking. Using the arithmetic from our cook-chill article, that means five or six 10-level cabinets, or two roll-in chillers that take trolleys straight from the combi ovens. The model uses roll-ins: fewer transfers, fewer hands.

Cold rooms keep raw materials, semi-finished and finished products apart, with at least two days of production in spare capacity. Deliveries can fail. Cold cannot.

### 5. Packing, washing, logistics

**Packing.** A tray sealer and a weigh-price labeller. Details in our separate packaging article.

**Washing.** About 3,000 crates and pans a day: the volume at which a tunnel washer starts to pay back against hand washing. We ran the numbers in our article on washing and sanitation.

**Logistics.** Two or three refrigerated vans on fixed routes, plus insulated containers. Logistics is a cold room on wheels and deserves the same rigour.

### 6. Staff

Illustratively, across two shifts:

- prep, hot and cold kitchens: 20–24 people;
- packing and dispatch: 6–8;
- washing: 4;
- food technologist and quality control: 2;
- maintenance engineer: 1–2;
- drivers: one per van.

About 35–40 people in total. In return, each outlet goes from three or four cooks per shift to one person who reheats, assembles and serves.

### 7. Illustrative budget

Numbers are illustrative, to show structure only. Say the process equipment costs 60 million roubles. It then splits roughly as follows:

- thermal (combi ovens, kettles, pans): 25–30%;
- refrigeration (blast chilling, cold rooms): 25–30%;
- mechanical (vegetable and meat processing): 10–15%;
- packing: 10–15%;
- washing: about 10%;
- neutral stainless (tables, racks, trolleys, pans): 5–10%.

A separate and often comparable line is building services and construction: ventilation, power, drainage with grease traps, floors, water treatment. Say another 20 million. Total project: 80 million roubles.

**Payback.** Each outlet frees three cooks at a fully loaded 90,000 roubles a month: 270,000 per outlet, 5.4 million across the chain. Subtract 38 central kitchen staff (3.4 million) and logistics (0.6 million). Net labour savings: 1.4 million a month. Add bulk purchasing (say 3% of 20 million roubles of monthly raw materials, or 0.6 million) and waste cut from 6% to 3% (another 0.6 million). Total: 2.6 million a month, payback of about 31 months. (Your numbers will differ, which is the whole point of calculating.)

Note that on labour alone the project takes almost five years to pay back. A central kitchen pays for itself through consistency, purchasing and waste. Fewer cooks is only one line.

### 8. How not to build a monument

The classic mistake is to build for 50 outlets “to grow into”. Ovens and kettles run at a third of capacity while depreciation runs at full. It is wiser to plan floor space and services for growth and add equipment as outlets open. This applies especially to blast chilling and packing, which scale in modules.
