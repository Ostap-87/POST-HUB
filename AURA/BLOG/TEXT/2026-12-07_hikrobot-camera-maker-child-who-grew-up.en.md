When Hikrobot comes up, someone always asks, “Isn’t that the camera company?” Not quite. The camera company is the parent. Hikrobot makes the things that drive around warehouses and stare at conveyor belts. Still, a surname is hard to shake, so this article keeps both in view: the family name, and the fact that the child moved out a while ago.

At Aura Robotics we source robots directly from Chinese factories, and Hikrobot comes up often in warehouse automation projects. So here is the company as we would assess it before a purchase: who owns it, what it builds, how big it is, and where the catches are.

### The family tree

Hikrobot is a subsidiary of Hikvision, one of the world’s largest video surveillance manufacturers. Hikvision holds 60% directly; an employee platform holds the other 40%. Hikvision itself is controlled by the state-owned China Electronics Technology Group (CETC), which holds roughly 39% through an intermediate entity, according to public sources.

A nesting doll, then: a state corporation, inside it a camera maker, inside that a robot maker. For a buyer this is not politics but procurement. A company owned like this will not vanish next year along with its website, as startups sometimes do. It does, however, have relatives not everyone wants to be seen with. More on that below.

### What they make

Three businesses: machine vision, mobile robots and robot arms.

**Machine vision** covers industrial cameras, code readers, 3D cameras and the VisionMaster software platform. The company says it has shipped more than 10 million vision devices and has over 600,000 users of its industrial software. At its April 2026 conference in Tonglu it launched more than 35 new products, including high-precision 3D and AI-enabled cameras.

**Mobile robots** come in four families, named, as is traditional, with acronyms: LMR (latent robots that slide under a rack or cart and carry it away), FMR (forklift robots for pallets and standard bins, using laser SLAM and vision navigation), CMR (robots with a conveyor deck for handing loads to a line) and CTU (case-handling robots that pull totes from racking and carry several at once).

Above them sits the RCS fleet management system. According to the company, by early 2021 more than a thousand of its robots were running at once in a single FAW Toyota plant. In total, more than 180,000 Hikrobot mobile robots have rolled off the line.

**Robot arms** are the newest line: Hikrobot launched a full range in 2024 and is still catching up. Vision and mobile robots bring in most of the revenue.

The point here is not navigation methods (we covered those separately). It is that the camera, the robot and the fleet software come from one family. When something breaks, nobody has to work out whose fault it is.

When one company builds the eyes, the legs and the brain, the blame meeting gets much shorter.

### The numbers: big, no longer explosive

Revenue went from 3.90 billion yuan in 2022 to 4.94 billion in 2023, 5.93 billion in 2024 and 6.45 billion in 2025. Net profit was about 640 million yuan in 2022 and about 800 million in 2023. This is a profitable business, not a pitch deck.

Growth did slow to 8.8% in 2025, which is fine for a mature industrial company and less fine for an IPO candidate. Listing documents put its 2023 share of China’s mobile robot market at 14.7%, with more than 17,000 customers in over 200 sub-sectors.

The IPO itself is a play in several acts. Hikvision announced the spin-off in December 2021. The Shenzhen ChiNext application was accepted in March 2023, aiming to raise 6 billion yuan. Then came the waltz: exchange questions, answers, suspension over stale financials, an update, back under review, suspended again. In April 2026, after another financial update, the application returned to review. Reported concerns centre on independence from the parent and related-party transactions.

Three years in the listing queue is not failure. It is proof that regulators, too, can go round in circles without any navigation at all.

### The surname and the sanctions

Hikvision has been on the US Entity List since 2019, with several subsidiaries added in 2023. Its equipment has been barred from sale and import in the US since 2022. The UK restricts its cameras at sensitive government sites, and in 2025 Canada ordered its local operation to close. Ukraine’s anti-corruption agency listed it as an “international sponsor of war” in 2023.

What does this mean for a Russian buyer of Hikrobot warehouse robots? Three sober points. The restrictions mainly target technology flowing into Hikvision and its products in Western markets; they do not apply to a Russian customer directly, though US-origin components and payment logistics can complicate things on the supplier side. A group that has lived with restrictions for years has usually localised its supply chain already, which helps continuity. And a large group with Western reputational exposure tends to be cautious in public about Russia, so warranty, firmware and spare parts terms belong in the contract in full detail, as with any manufacturer.

Sanctions do not make the robot worse. They make the contract longer.

### Who it suits

Hikrobot is strongest where you need many identical machines under one fleet system, with machine vision next to them: code reading, completeness checks, [sorting](/en/catalog/sorting). With dozens of robots and cameras on the line, a single-vendor stack saves integration time.

If you need one or two robots to tow carts, a system built for a thousand machines may be overkill, like hiring a symphony orchestra to play “Happy Birthday”.

Either way, the calculation decides, not the logo. We select mobile robots and cameras for the task, calculate payback, show the specific unit with its serial number on video from the factory floor, and test it before shipment. One team runs the project from selection to start-up, so “why can’t the camera read the code” never ends up stranded between three contractors.

### The bottom line

Hikrobot grew from a division of a big company into a manufacturer with more than six billion yuan in revenue. It is still waiting for its stock market debut, and it still carries a surname that makes the West twitch.

Children of famous parents spend their lives proving they are their own people. Sometimes they manage it. The warehouse, at least, never asks for a surname.
