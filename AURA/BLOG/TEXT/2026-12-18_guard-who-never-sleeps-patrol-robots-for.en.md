The night patrol is a genre everyone who has worked at a plant knows. A guard with a torch walks the fence, checks the gates, shines a light into the dark corner behind the warehouse, finds nobody and moves on. Two hours later he does it again. Somewhere around 4 a.m. the round gets shorter and the corner gets darker.

That is no criticism of the guard. It is biology. At 4 a.m. a person is meant to be asleep, and the body says so with growing insistence. A robot at 4 a.m. has no idea it is 4 a.m.

At Aura Robotics we source robots directly from Chinese factories and match them to the job. Patrol robots are a class of their own, easily confused with a neighbour, so let us start there.

### Security is not inspection

An [inspection](/en/catalog/inspection) robot looks at equipment: gauges, transformer temperature, leaks, vibration. A patrol robot looks at people and events: who came through the gate, who is standing by the fence, whose car is in the fire lane, who is smoking by the store of flammables.

The platforms can be identical; the jobs are not. That means different sensors, different software and different legal questions. When an inspection robot films a gauge, the gauge does not object. A patrol robot films people, and the law has opinions about that.

### Which robots patrol

**Wheeled patrol robots** are the most common choice for flat ground: paved yards, logistics parks, car parks. Four or six wheels, a mast with a pan-tilt camera, often a thermal camera, a loudspeaker and lidar for navigation. They follow a route and return to charge on their own. Their enemies are kerbs, steps, deep snow and mud.

**Quadrupeds** go where wheels struggle: stairs, kerbs, gravel, rough ground. A recent example: in January 2026 China’s DEEP Robotics described security deployments of its wheel-legged LYNX M20. According to the company, it patrols at up to 2 m/s, carries a 96-line lidar with a 360°×90° field of view, is rated IP66, operates from −20 to +55 °C, runs about 2.5 hours per battery and supports autonomous battery swapping. It flags badly parked cars, smoking and people loitering in restricted areas, sends real-time alerts to the control room and can issue spoken warnings. Sites named include an industrial park in Guangzhou, the Yiwu wholesale market and logistics parks abroad.

**Heavy patrol platforms.** At the 2025 World AI Conference in Shanghai, the 21st Research Institute of China Electronics Technology Group showed Piaoqi, a patrol robot for industrial parks and power facilities that plans its own routes, runs outdoors for two to four hours and can work in coordinated fleets.

Note those battery numbers: two, two and a half, four hours. The night is longer. So “how does the robot last until morning?” is one of the first questions in any project.

### What robots do well, and what they don’t

They are good at walking the same route with no shortcuts, timestamping every checkpoint, seeing in the dark with thermal imaging, noticing change (an open gate, a person where nobody should be at night), streaming video to the control room and forgetting nothing.

They are bad at detaining intruders (they cannot and should not), handling unusual situations, talking to a lost driver at the wrong gate, and wading through knee-deep snow.

So a patrol robot does not replace security; it changes the job. The guard stops walking in circles and starts responding to what the robot finds. Press releases call this a “human-robot joint patrol model”. At the gatehouse it is simpler: the robot walks, the human drives.

A robot is not afraid of the dark. It also cannot arrest anyone in it.

### Fitting a robot into existing security

**Control room and CCTV.** A robot is one more camera, only mobile. Its video and alerts should reach the same place as your fixed cameras and perimeter sensors. Ask which protocols it supports for video and events, and whether it connects to your video management system rather than only to its own app.

**The security contractor.** In Russia, guarding services are provided by licensed security companies, and for them a robot is a technical aid, like a camera or sensor. Your contract should say who is responsible for the robot and who responds to its alerts, and when.

**On-site connectivity.** The robot needs reliable radio along the whole route: Wi-Fi, private LTE or similar. The dead zone behind the far warehouse is exactly where coverage matters most.

**Charging and weather.** Where the charger stands, whether it is protected from snow, what the robot does in heavy rain, blizzards and ice, and the temperature at which it stops. In most of Russia the lower end of the operating range is not a theoretical question.

### Video and personal data

A patrol robot records people, so it handles their images. A few principles worth knowing before launch.

In Russia, the data protection regulator’s position is that video recorded on a guarded site is not biometric data processing until the images are used to identify someone. Once the system matches faces against a database, for example for face-based entry or searching for a specific person, it becomes biometrics, and Article 11 of Federal Law 152-FZ requires written consent, with exceptions set out in the law.

Signs warning of video surveillance should be posted. The purposes of surveillance, retention periods and who has access should be fixed in internal policies, and employees whose workplaces come under surveillance must be told in advance.

Then there is storage. If the robot sends recordings to the manufacturer’s cloud abroad by default, find out before you buy and, as a rule, configure storage on your own server. Take this to a lawyer: we describe general principles, not legal advice.

Facial recognition is the feature that is easiest to switch on and hardest to explain to an inspector.

### Calculating payback

The classic mistake is assuming the robot replaces a guard. Usually it replaces the rounds, not the post. An honest model asks how many hours of patrols people do each day, how many the robot can take, how the shift changes, and what the robot, charger, connectivity, CCTV integration and maintenance cost.

An illustrative example: if several people work the night shift and half their time goes on rounds, the robot may allow a smaller shift or move people to response and gate control. A bonus is a patrol log with exact times and video, which manual rounds rarely produce. Real numbers depend on the site, and we calculate them for each project.

### The bottom line

The old village watchman walked the streets at night banging a wooden clapper so thieves knew someone was awake. A patrol robot does the same, only its clapper has a thermal camera. And the person who used to walk the fence all night now sits somewhere warm deciding what to do about what the robot found. That is security too. Just a more grown-up kind.
