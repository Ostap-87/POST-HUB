The usual way to make a robot goes like this. One company casts the housing, another makes the gearbox, a third the motor, a fourth the servo drive, a fifth the controller, and a sixth puts its logo on the result and calls itself a robot maker. Nothing wrong with that; cars and planes are built the same way.

Sometimes, though, a company spends twenty years making motors, drives and controllers for other people’s robots, looks around and wonders why it is the one not putting its logo on the front. That is roughly how Inovance got into robotics.

At Aura Robotics we buy equipment directly from Chinese factories, and that story is exactly why Inovance interests us. Here is who they are and what it means for a buyer.

### Who they are

Shenzhen Inovance Technology was founded in Shenzhen in 2003. Its founder, Zhu Xingming, and part of the early team came from Huawei Electric, Huawei’s power electronics unit, sold to Emerson in 2001. Telecom roots, engineering temperament.

Today it is one of China’s biggest industrial automation companies, covering general automation (inverters, servo systems, PLCs and HMIs, motors, sensors, machine vision and industrial robots), EV powertrains, elevator control systems and rail transit components. Essentially, a list of everything in a modern economy that moves. The robot is not the biggest item on it, but it is the most telling.

### The numbers

Operating revenue reached about 45 billion yuan in 2025, up from 36.9 billion in 2024, with net profit of around 5.05 billion. In the first half of 2026 revenue rose 20% to 24.7 billion yuan.

The mix is more interesting than the total. In H1 2026, according to Shenwan Hongyuan analysts, general automation brought in about 11 billion yuan: roughly 4.8 billion from servo systems, 3.3 billion from inverters and 1.4 billion from PLCs and HMIs. “Emerging businesses”, including robotics and digital energy, were about 1.5 billion, nearly double the year before.

Inovance leads China’s servo market, though estimates vary by method: 15.9% according to Gongkong, ahead of Yaskawa, Delta, Panasonic and Mitsubishi, or 28% by the figure the company cites. In SCARA robots it is also China’s number one, with about a quarter of the market (27% by company data, about 25% in analysts’ H1 2026 estimate). Across all industrial robots in China, the same analysts rank it third with about 9%, and second among domestic makers.

If you sell motors to your competitors, you know more about them than they know about themselves.

Inovance has traded on Shenzhen’s ChiNext since 2010. In January 2026 it announced plans for a Hong Kong listing as well; Bloomberg and China Daily reported Bank of America, CICC, Guotai Junan International and Morgan Stanley as arrangers, with up to 2 billion dollars under discussion. The status may well have moved on by the time you read this, so check HKEX.

### The robot range

**SCARA** is the core: IR-S4, IR-S7 and IR-S10, the ceiling-mounted IR-TS4 and IR-TS5, and the heavier IR-S25, IR-S35 and IR-S60, covering 4 to 60 kg and 350 to 1,200 mm of reach. We have explained elsewhere why a 0.35-second catalogue cycle does not mean 170 cycles a minute on your line.

**Six-axis** robots are the IR-R series, from the compact IR-R4 (4 kg, about 550 mm) to the IR-R16 with 2,107 mm reach and the 25 kg IR-R25. These are for assembly, machine tending and light processing, not 200 kg body-shop welders.

**The controller** is the IRCB501 series, with Modbus TCP, EtherNet/IP, EtherCAT for external axes, and optional PROFINET and CC-Link; an IP54 version is available.

We found no [cobots](/en/catalog/cobots) in Inovance’s European robot catalogue. The company is, however, developing humanoid components: seven-axis bionic arms, planetary and linear actuators, frameless torque motors and planetary roller screws. A bet on humanoids, then, but as a parts supplier rather than an assembler.

### What vertical integration buys you

**Spare parts.** When a servo drive or motor fails, a manufacturer with its own drives replaces it from its own stock. An assembler waits for its supplier, who may have discontinued the model. For a robot expected to last ten years, that matters.

**Compatibility.** If your line already runs Inovance inverters and PLCs, and Chinese automation is increasingly common in Russian plants, a robot from the same maker is easier to fit in: one programming environment, one diagnostic logic, one supplier.

**Cost.** A company that makes the key and costliest parts itself (motors, drives, controller) can hold prices down. Not a guarantee of cheapness, but it helps explain how Inovance took the SCARA lead so quickly.

The flip side: Inovance is a giant, and robotics is one line in its report. Its big customers are carmakers and battery makers, not a workshop with ten machines. So it matters who will support your particular project, and how fast.

A big company is not always interested in a small customer. But a big company definitely has the spare parts.

### Who it suits

Inovance makes sense for SCARA work in electronics assembly, small-item packing and sorting, and wherever its automation already runs the plant. For heavy palletizing, body welding or collaborative work next to people, look at other makers.

### The bottom line

For twenty years the company made what goes inside other people’s robots. Then it put it all in its own housing and became number one in its segment within a few years.

Engineering is a bit like cooking: grow your own vegetables long enough and one day you open a restaurant. Not necessarily the best in town. But it will never run out of salad.
