> There’s no such thing as coffee that’s too strong. There is such a thing as an accountant who’s too honest. — A thought I had over my third cup

I drink a lot of coffee and enjoy none of it. It’s medicine against meetings. So I treat coffee chains the way I treat pharmacies: no love, daily visits. And of all the pharmacies in the world, I have one favorite story. It’s about a coffee chain that first fooled the world and then kept it caffeinated.

Luckin Coffee was founded in Beijing in October 2017. By May 2019 it was trading on Nasdaq at $17 a share, having raised about $645 million. Eighteen months from first store to opening bell. Some people take longer to pick wallpaper.

The model was simple and very Chinese: tiny pickup counters, ordering only through the app, discounts, coupons, first cup free, second cup nearly free. Luckin opened stores faster than people could get used to them and lost money faster than it opened stores. Nobody minded. In the late 2010s, losses were called “investing in market share,” which, to be fair, sounds much more upbeat.

A loss renamed a strategy becomes a strategy. Until the first audit.

## Until the first audit

On January 31, 2020, short seller Carson Block’s Muddy Waters published an anonymous report claiming Luckin’s sales were heavily inflated. Luckin denied everything. Two months later, on April 2, it announced that an internal investigation had found that COO Liu Jian and employees reporting to him had fabricated about 2.2 billion yuan — roughly $310 million — of 2019 sales.

The stock fell about 76% in a day. In May, both CEO Jenny Qian Zhiya and Liu Jian were fired. On June 29, 2020, Luckin was delisted from Nasdaq. In December it agreed to pay the SEC $180 million. In February 2021 it filed for Chapter 15 bankruptcy protection in New York.

I’ve spent many years in marketing, and I know everyone polishes their numbers. But usually it’s the way people polish their height on a dating profile — an inch or so. This was a profile so polished that a different person showed up for the date.

## Minutes that never existed

An intermission. Here’s what the minutes of the year-end board meeting might have looked like, had anyone kept them honestly.

*MINUTES No. 1. Board meeting on 2019 revenue*

*Heard: a report on sales growth.*

*Spoke: the auditors. Asked where the sales came from.*

*Spoke: the COO. Said the sales came from customers.*

*Spoke: the auditors. Asked where the customers came from.*

*Resolved: drink coffee, recount sales, dismiss COO.*

*Dissenting opinion: there were customers. Just not all of them.*

Everything in these minutes is true except that they ever existed. I made them up. That’s the only made-up document in this essay, and unlike some people, I’m disclosing it right away.

## In the reports, not in the cups

This is usually where the story ends. The company disappears, the founders give interviews about being misunderstood, a journalist writes a book, and the book sells better than the company did.

But something strange happened. The stores stayed open. People kept drinking coffee. The fraud was in the reports, not in the cups. Strip the creative accounting out of the company, and what remained was a perfectly viable business: an app, stores, suppliers, and millions of people who need to wake up in the morning.

Control passed to Centurium Capital, an early backer that put in another $240 million in spring 2021. Founder and chairman Lu Zhengyao was gone by the summer of 2020. A $460 million debt restructuring went through, and the company exited bankruptcy in 2022. CEO Guo Jinyi later put it better than I could: “Today’s Luckin Coffee has transformed into an essentially new company — with only the name unchanged.”

The name stayed. Everything else had to be brewed again.

## The villain becomes the most likable character

What came next played out like a bad TV show in which the villain somehow becomes the most likable character.

In the summer of 2023, Luckin’s quarterly revenue in China overtook that of Starbucks China for the first time. By the end of 2023 it was the country’s largest coffee chain. On September 4, 2023, together with baijiu maker Moutai, it launched a latte laced with that famously strong liquor. It sold more than 5.42 million cups on day one, for over 100 million yuan.

Coffee with baijiu. A company caught embellishing reality started selling a drink that embellishes reality all by itself. That’s not a failure or a comeback anymore. That’s a sense of humor.

By the end of 2025, Luckin had 31,048 stores and full-year revenue of 49.3 billion yuan, up 43%. Its 30,000th store opened in Shenzhen. It went abroad: Singapore, Malaysia and — touchingly — New York, where its first two Manhattan stores opened in summer 2025, in the very country whose regulator once fined it. In March 2026, Centurium reportedly agreed to buy Blue Bottle from Nestlé. Luckin says it wants to relist in the US, but gives no timetable.

In most places, returning to the scene of the crime is considered a mistake. At Luckin, it’s called “entering the US market.”

One more thing. The former founders didn’t vanish either. In 2022, Lu Zhengyao and Jenny Qian launched a new chain, Cotti Coffee: almost the same model, almost the same look, almost the same coupons. A price war followed, and a cup in China dropped to around nine yuan.

No playwright would have dared. The people who broke the company are competing with the company that was fixed without them. And it’s hard to say which of them Chinese customers are more grateful to. Probably both — coffee got cheaper.

## The most honest product this company makes

I spend a lot of time in negotiations with Chinese partners, and I drink a lot of coffee there. It’s almost always a cup from some chain nobody in Moscow has heard of. Sometimes it’s Luckin. And every time I catch myself thinking that I’m holding the most honest product this company makes. Numbers in a report can be drawn. Coffee in a cup can’t. People either drink it or they don’t.

Every failure has a part that works. If you’re brave enough to throw out the rest, you can start over from there.

As for the minutes of that board meeting, I’ll never see them. That’s fine. Let something in this story remain unproven.
