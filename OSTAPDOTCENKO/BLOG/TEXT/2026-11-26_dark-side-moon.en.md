Moonshot AI’s Chinese name translates as “The Dark Side of the Moon,” reportedly after the Pink Floyd album, and it was founded in the record’s fiftieth-anniversary year. Its Beijing meeting rooms, judging by staff photos, are named after rock bands: Led Zeppelin, Deep Purple, The Rolling Stones, Metallica.

I’m from a different musical generation and a different country, but I know one thing for sure. If your conference room is called Metallica, your meetings are never short.

The name turned out to be prophetic. The moon has a side everyone sees and a side nobody sees. Kimi has lived on both.

## Two million characters

The company was founded in Beijing in 2023 by Yang Zhilin: Tsinghua graduate, Carnegie Mellon PhD, former researcher at Google Brain and Meta AI. In October 2023 it launched Kimi, a chatbot with a simple trick: it could read about 200,000 Chinese characters at once. By spring 2024, two million.

Two million characters is several fat novels at once. Upload a 300-page contract, an annual report and the lawyers’ emails, and the machine keeps all of it in mind. I can’t keep track of my reading glasses. Kimi could keep track of every clause in someone else’s contract.

In February 2024 Moonshot raised more than a billion dollars in a round led by Alibaba, Xiaohongshu and HongShan. The company wasn’t even a year old. At that age, people get a rattle. Kimi got a billion.

## The bright side of the moon

Then came the bright side of the moon: advertising.

According to Chinese business media, at its peak Kimi was spending nearly 200 million yuan a month on ads. One investor estimated each new user cost about 10 yuan, or 12 to 13 including compute. Kimi was everywhere: video apps, search, the gap between clips. Around October 2024 it passed 36 million monthly active users.

I’ve been in advertising a long time, and professionally speaking, this was textbook performance marketing. The numbers rose, the dashboards turned green, the investor decks wrote themselves.

There’s just one problem with a user you bought for ten yuan. Someone else can buy them for nine.

## Long context, short attention span

Then came January 20, 2025. That day Moonshot released its reasoning model Kimi k1.5, claiming it matched OpenAI’s o1 in math and coding.

The same day, DeepSeek released R1.

You know the rest. R1 was open, free, and loud without a single yuan spent on ads. Users went where attention arrives without being paid for. Moonshot cut its ad and app-store spending. According to QuestMobile, Kimi’s monthly users fell from 14 million in Q2 2025 to 9.9 million in Q3. By December it had dropped from second place among AI assistants to seventh.

Long context, short attention span. Not the machine’s — ours.

## The dark side of the moon

And this is where the dark side of the moon begins: the side you can’t see from Earth and nobody was really watching.

In July 2025 Moonshot released Kimi K2, a trillion-parameter open-weights model. In November came K2 Thinking, and with it a headline number: training had supposedly cost $4.6 million. In a Reddit Q&A, Yang Zhilin said that was “not an official number” and that cost is hard to pin down because most of it is research and experiments.

It’s the first time I’ve seen a founder push back on a number for being too low. Usually it’s the other way round.

After that, things moved fast. K2.5 in January 2026, K2.6 in April. According to Chinese media, annual recurring revenue passed $100 million after K2.5 and $200 million by April, mostly from subscriptions and API. No more money going out on ads. Money started coming in.

In May 2026 Moonshot raised about $2 billion at a valuation above $20 billion, in a round led by Meituan’s Long-Z fund. In total, according to press reports, it has raised more than 37 billion yuan.

In July 2026 came Kimi K3: 2.8 trillion parameters, weights released on July 27. On the Artificial Analysis index it placed fourth out of 189, the top open-weights score.

## Echoes last longer

And there’s one small detail in the license that I, as a marketer, read three times. If a product built on K3 has more than 100 million monthly users or $20 million in monthly revenue, it must prominently display “Kimi K3.”

Now that’s a pivot. The company used to pay 10 yuan to be seen. Now it gives the model away, and the license makes sure it’s seen.

Paid advertising is a shout. Your name inside someone else’s product is an echo. Echoes last longer.

I’ve watched plenty of Chinese companies buy growth first and figure out what to do with it later. Kimi did it louder than most and paid more than most. But it may be the only one that, having lost on the bright side, quietly moved to the dark side and got to work.

The moon, by the way, always shows us the same face. We don’t notice, because we only look where the light is.

The other side is there. It just doesn’t make the evening news.
