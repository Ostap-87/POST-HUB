I’ve spent twenty years selling other people’s products and telling clients that a good product sells itself. It doesn’t. But clients like hearing it, and I get paid precisely because it isn’t true.

ByteDance seems to be the only company that took this fairy tale literally. And it named its AI accordingly: Doubao. In Chinese, 豆包 is a steamed bun with sweet bean filling. Not “Mind,” not “Oracle,” not “Infinity.” A bun.

My first thought was: bad naming. Then I looked at the numbers and realized the bad call was mine. This wasn’t a bun. It was the Gingerbread Man.

You know the story. Someone bakes him, puts him by the window to cool, and off he runs. ByteDance baked Doubao in 2023 — back then the model was called Skylark, and the chatbot went public that August. In May 2024 the company’s cloud arm, Volcano Engine, unveiled a whole family of Doubao models. And off he ran.

In May 2024 Doubao processed 120 billion text tokens a day. In March 2026 Volcano Engine reported 120 trillion. Same number, a thousand times bigger. The bun didn’t grow. It became a bakery.

By the end of 2025 the Chinese business press, citing several sources, reported over 100 million daily users. QuestMobile counted 382 million monthly users in the first half of 2026. Alibaba’s Qwen had 167 million, DeepSeek 130 million. Put together, still smaller than one bun.

Run, run, as fast as you can. You can’t catch me.

How did ByteDance pull it off? The same way it built TikTok. That’s the whole point of this essay; you can stop reading now. Please don’t — I worked hard on the rest.

ByteDance has one recipe: the product is free, it’s everywhere, and it asks for nothing but your time. Douyin never explained why you should watch fifteen-second videos. It just showed you the next one. Doubao doesn’t explain why you should talk to it. It just answers — in text, voice, pictures, video, or a poem for your boss’s birthday.

Meanwhile, rivals did what any marketer does when out of ideas: they bought ads. According to AppGrowing data cited by Yicai, ten major Chinese AI apps spent about 1.5 billion yuan on advertising from January to mid-November 2024. Kimi alone spent 220 million in October. Tencent’s push for Yuanbao reportedly approached 6 billion yuan by July 2025.

And a ByteDance insider told KrAsia that of all the company’s products to cross 100 million daily users, Doubao got there with the most modest marketing spend.

As a marketer, I take that personally. It’s like the Gingerbread Man outrunning the fox without paying her a single compliment.

Then there was the holiday. Chinese New Year 2026 kicked off what people called the “Spring Festival AI war.” Tencent handed out about a billion yuan in red envelopes through Yuanbao. Alibaba announced a campaign of nearly three billion for Qwen. Baidu, around half a billion.

ByteDance behaved like a guest who brings no gift but takes the head of the table. Volcano Engine became the AI cloud partner of CCTV’s Spring Festival Gala, the most-watched broadcast in China. Doubao was on air; viewers drew prizes and ordered greeting cards. That evening saw 1.9 billion AI interactions, with a peak of more than 63 billion tokens per minute.

Sixty-three billion a minute. I haven’t said that many words in my whole life, and talking is my job.

The best advertising is the kind the audience pays for. With attention.

There was one moment when the bun rolled the wrong way. In December 2025 ZTE released the nubia M153 phone with a built-in “Doubao mobile assistant”: a limited run of 30,000 units at 3,499 yuan. The assistant could tap buttons inside other people’s apps — order, pay, forward.

The app owners were not amused. WeChat, Taobao, Alipay and banking apps started blocking it.

Even a gingerbread man knows: you don’t roll into someone else’s house without knocking. Especially a house where the money is kept.

Now, the fox. Everyone in the story is waiting for her, except the Gingerbread Man.

In May 2026 ByteDance began testing Doubao subscriptions at 68, 200 and 500 yuan a month. The free version stays, but heavy tasks — slide decks, data analysis, video — will cost money. A hundred and twenty trillion tokens a day isn’t just fame. It’s also a bill for chips and electricity. How Chinese companies first crashed AI prices and then started raising them is a story for another essay.

I’m not gloating. I sympathize. I’ve told clients for years: “Free isn’t a price. It’s a phase.” I never fully believed it myself.

The fox doesn’t always come from the woods. Sometimes she comes from Finance.

Then again, maybe this story ends differently. ByteDance has done this before: hook a huge audience on something free, then gently sell them what they can no longer live without — first videos, then ads, then a shop inside the feed. Doubao, according to Chinese media, is already learning to sell right inside the conversation.

I look at this bun with respect and a little envy. I once had a simple free product too: advice. I gave it to anyone who asked. Nobody came back for a subscription.

The Gingerbread Man sang his song to everyone he met. Doubao sings it to 382 million people a month. Same song: you can’t catch me.

We know how the old story ends. Nobody knows how this one will. Which may be the most honest thing anyone can say about AI right now.
