Articles about Chinese startups come in two flavors: “They’ll beat everyone” and “They’re all doomed.” I’ve read both, occasionally about the same company, six months apart, and confess I agreed with each at the time. I have an excellent memory for numbers and a poor one for my own forecasts.

Today I’ll try a third flavor. They’re all alive. They just aren’t all tigers anymore.

## The Six Little Tigers

First, the menagerie. In 2023 China was in its “battle of a hundred models”: anyone with a GPU cluster and a press office released a large language model. By early 2024 six startups had emerged from the crowd, each worth over a billion dollars. The press called them the “Six Little Tigers,” a nod to the Four Asian Tigers that once raced to catch up with the West.

Zhipu and Moonshot (maker of the Kimi chatbot) in Beijing, along with Baichuan and 01.AI. MiniMax and StepFun in Shanghai. Backed by Alibaba, Tencent, Meituan, venture funds and city governments.

Note the word “little.” These were cubs. You could pet them. For a while.

## A cage built from three sides at once

Then someone built a cage, from three sides at once.

Wall one: the 2024 price war, when the giants cut model prices by orders of magnitude. Wall two: the giants themselves — ByteDance with Doubao, Alibaba with Qwen, Tencent, Baidu — with their own clouds, billions of users and marketing budgets, giving AI away for free. Wall three: DeepSeek. In January 2025 a small Hangzhou team nobody had counted among the tigers released an open model on par with the world’s best and rattled the markets.

It’s hard to sell what your neighbor gives away. It’s harder still when the neighbor’s freebie is better.

In a cage, it isn’t the strongest tiger that survives. It’s the first one to realize it’s a cage.

## Tonight’s program

From here on, it’s a circus. I can see the poster.

> **TONIGHT’S PROGRAM** *Part One: Jumping Through Hoops* 1. Zhipu the Tiger. First AI model maker in the world to leap onto the stock exchange. Drumroll. 2. MiniMax the Tigress. Same act, the next day. Standing ovation, mostly on tour abroad. 3. Moonshot the Tiger. Taking a run-up. Jump announced. 4. StepFun the Tiger. Warming up backstage. *Part Two: Career Change* 5. Baichuan the Tiger: “The Doctor Will See You Now.” 6. 01.AI the Tiger: “Training Other People’s Tigers.” *Intermission. Snack bar open. Snack prices have gone up.*

Act by act.

**Zhipu** listed in Hong Kong on January 8, 2026, at HK$116.2 a share, billed as the world’s first publicly traded large-model company. In June its market value briefly topped HK$1 trillion intraday; by September 1 it had settled at HK$557 billion. First-half 2026 revenue: 954 million yuan, nearly five times a year earlier. Loss: about 2 billion. Zhipu raised prices twice that year — 30% on its coding plan in February, another 8–17% in April.

A tiger that raised ticket prices and still filled the tent. That’s rare. Usually only the cotton candy gets more expensive.

**MiniMax** jumped the next day, January 9. Its retail tranche was oversubscribed more than 1,800 times, and the shares more than doubled on day one. Market value peaked at about HK$263 billion in late May and slid back to around HK$100 billion by autumn. Over 70% of its revenue comes from abroad. A tiger that performs more on tour than at home.

**Moonshot** raised about $2 billion in spring 2026 at a valuation above $20 billion, led by Meituan. In September Reuters reported a confidential Hong Kong IPO filing targeting around $3 billion. Kimi deserves its own story; here, just the jump.

**StepFun**, founded in 2023 by former Microsoft vice president Jiang Daxin, closed a $2.5 billion round in May 2026 and restructured for a listing. Still warming up. Judging by the warm-up, it plans to jump high.

## Knowing when to change careers

I like Part Two better. It’s about people who know when to change careers.

**Baichuan** was founded by Wang Xiaochuan, creator of the Sogou search engine. It was the first tiger to leave the general race for healthcare. In January 2026 it open-sourced Baichuan-M3, a medical model that, by the company’s account, topped the HealthBench test. It has about 3 billion yuan in the bank, says the founder, and may list in 2027.

**01.AI** was founded by Kai-Fu Lee, one of the best-known names in Chinese AI. After DeepSeek, he publicly conceded that a startup couldn’t win the foundation-model race — almost an act of heroism in our profession. Now 01.AI no longer trains its own large models. It fine-tunes others’ — DeepSeek, Qwen, Zhipu’s GLM — and deploys them for corporate clients. About 240 employees, half the business outside China, an IPO targeted for 2027.

A tiger turned tamer is still in the circus. He’s just holding the whip now instead of the tail.

## Not a prison but a show schedule

The result? Not one of the six died. Four are jumping through hoops; two changed acts. For an industry that had over a hundred models in 2023, that’s an almost indecently good outcome.

The secret seems simple. Those still in the big game learned to charge money: for model access, for subscriptions, for work abroad. Those who left found places the giants don’t go — yet. The cage turned out to be not a prison but a show schedule.

It’s not the loudest roar that survives. It’s knowing when your act is on.

They say when the circus leaves town, the clowns stay. Here the circus never left. The tigers are still here. And so are the clowns — us forecasters, who two years ago were confidently sorting the six into survivors and goners. Thankfully, I never said my forecast out loud. But it existed, and it was grim.

Frankly, I enjoy being wrong. It’s a rare case where reality turned out kinder than my forecast.
