> The early bird gets the worm. — Proverb. The second mouse gets the cheese.

Some people are always first in line. They show up the night before, write their number on their hand, and stand at the door till morning. Then the shop opens, and it turns out the delivery went to the shop next door, where the line started later but in the right place.

I’m a night-before person myself. So I feel about Baidu the way you feel about a fellow sufferer.

Just how early was Baidu? In May 2014 it hired Andrew Ng, a Stanford professor and one of the best-known names in machine learning, to lead its research. Back then, most business people I knew thought a neural network was something to do with medicine.

In March 2019 Baidu released its ERNIE language model, which beat Google’s BERT on Chinese language tasks. That was three and a half years before ChatGPT. Baidu wasn’t just ahead. It was so far ahead nobody could see it.

The early bird gets up in the dark. Nobody notices it there.

Then came November 2022 and ChatGPT, and all of China turned to look at Baidu. Who else, if not the pioneer?

On March 16, 2023, founder and CEO Robin Li took the stage in Beijing to unveil Ernie Bot. Not live, though: he talked over a pre-recorded video. Baidu’s Hong Kong shares fell as much as 10% that day, wiping out more than $4 billion in value.

Li said something honest: “I can’t say we are fully ready.” And then: none of the Big Tech firms globally has made it yet; “Baidu is the first one.”

Both statements were true. That’s the awkward thing about the truth. Sometimes it’s about being first and about not being ready, at the same time.

Being first didn’t mean being alone. The users came. Baidu reported 100 million Ernie Bot users in December 2023, 200 million in April 2024 and 300 million that June. From November 2023 the premium version cost 59.9 yuan a month.

And here, I think, Baidu made its biggest mistake. Not a technical one, a marketing one. It decided that being first meant being paid. That the model should stay closed and be sold like any good product.

A short chronicle. Quotes and dates only, no embellishment.

April 2024, an internal memo from Robin Li, as reported by Chinese media: open-source models “make little sense.” “Being closed source allows us to make money, and only by making money can we attract computational resources and talent.”

May 2024. DeepSeek prices its API at 1 yuan per million tokens; Zhipu and ByteDance cut, Alibaba slashes by 97%. Four hours after Alibaba, Baidu makes two of its models free.

February 2025, Dubai, the World Governments Summit, right after the DeepSeek moment. Robin Li: “Innovation can’t be planned.” And: open source “can help you get more attention.”

March 2025. Ernie Bot goes free ahead of its April 1 schedule, alongside a new reasoning model, X1, priced at half of DeepSeek R1.

Summer 2025. Baidu opens the weights of Ernie 4.5.

One year from “closed source lets us make money” to “open source gets you attention.” I’m not mocking. I’m jealous. It usually takes me five years and someone else’s anniversary party to admit I was wrong.

Now the money, and here the picture is sad and instructive at once, like your own medical checkup.

Baidu made most of its money from search ads. You search, Baidu shows you ads, the advertiser pays. Then people stopped searching and started asking. A chatbot answers straight away, with no ten blue links and no ads in between.

In Q2 2026 Baidu’s revenue fell 4%, its fifth straight quarterly decline. Online marketing revenue dropped 19% to 13.1 billion yuan. Meanwhile AI-powered business revenue rose 25% to 12.5 billion. AI cloud infrastructure grew 50%, GPU cloud nearly quadrupled. Its chip unit, Kunlunxin, is being spun off for a Hong Kong listing.

Two lines are almost touching: one heading down, one heading up. With one hand Baidu loses ad revenue; with the other it sells cloud capacity to the very services taking that revenue away.

A search engine that spent its life helping people find answers is now searching for one itself.

Baidu hasn’t gone anywhere, mind you. Its main app had 644 million monthly users in June 2026. Its Apollo Go robotaxis run in 28 cities. In November 2025 it unveiled Ernie 5.0. This isn’t a loser. It’s a strong runner who has been looking at other people’s backs for a while now.

Among the AI apps that QuestMobile placed in its top tier in 2026 were Doubao, Qwen and DeepSeek. Ernie wasn’t there.

Being first is an honor. Unfortunately, cashiers don’t accept honor.

I spent a long time looking for the moral of this story and decided not to draw one. Let it stand where it is.

I’ll just say this. Someone has to get up in the dark and light the stove. The ones who wake up later find the kitchen warm and the pan already hot. They make the pancakes. Nobody remembers who lit the fire.

The early bird lights the stove. The others get breakfast.

That’s fine. Somebody has to get up early.
