Some holidays grow longer with age. Christmas in Western retail now begins somewhere around Halloween. China’s Singles’ Day has taken the same road, only faster and with much better logistics.

It started modestly. In 2009, Taobao Mall, now [Tmall](/en/companies/tmall), ran a one-day sale on 11 November. The date was chosen for its four ones, a nod to single life, which shoppers were invited to treat with retail therapy. The therapy worked so well that the patient list now runs past a billion and the course lasts more than a month.

We are writing this before the 2026 sale and do not know its results. What we can see clearly is what the event itself has become. That is arguably more interesting than any headline number, particularly since, as it turns out, there is no longer an official headline number.

### The scoreboard goes dark

For years the main spectacle of 11 November was not what people bought but how much. [Alibaba](/en/companies/alibaba) put the running total on a giant screen, and the counter spun like a slot machine where everyone won except the wallets. In 2021 the Tmall scoreboard stopped at 540.3 billion yuan.

In 2022 the scoreboard went dark. For the first time, Alibaba and JD did not publish total sales. Alibaba said only that results were “in line with last year’s GMV performance despite the macro challenges”, and counted brands that had doubled their sales. That has been the format ever since: companies report how many brands crossed 100 million yuan and leave the grand total to analysts.

When a number stops being flattering, it stops being shown. Retail did not invent this rule, but it applies it well.

### Someone else does the counting

Into the gap stepped independent data firms, above all Syntun. Their figures are unofficial and cover all platforms at once, but they are what the market has.

By Syntun’s count, the 2024 sale generated 1.4418 trillion yuan, up 26.6%. The “day” was no longer a day: Tmall, JD and [Pinduoduo](/en/companies/pinduoduo) sold from 14 October to 11 November, Douyin started on 8 October and Kuaishou on 10 October. Livestream commerce accounted for 332.5 billion yuan, instant delivery for 28.1 billion.

For 2025 Syntun reported 1.695 trillion yuan and growth of 14.2%. The small print: its counting window opened on 7 October, seven days longer than the year before. Tmall’s sale opened on 20 October at 8 p.m., and eMarketer put the whole season at five weeks against 28 days in 2024.

At a week a year, Singles’ Day will meet Christmas coming the other way sometime in the 2040s.

### Instant delivery: the sale leaves the warehouse

The big novelty of 2025 came from couriers rather than coupons. Over the summer, China’s platforms fought an instant delivery war, which we have covered separately, and in the autumn they brought it to Singles’ Day.

Alibaba launched its instant delivery service, Taobao Shangou, about four months before the sale. By the time the sale began, the company said, it was peaking at 120 million orders a day. For the event, 37,000 brands and 400,000 stores signed up with promises of delivery in minutes. Syntun estimated that Meituan Shangou, Taobao Shangou and JD Miaosong together sold 67 billion yuan over the season, up 138.4%.

Against the total, that is small. The point is elsewhere. A sale used to be something you waited for, ordered in advance and received days later. Now it arrives before dinner. A shopper who will not wait three days for a parcel will not wait three paragraphs for an advert either.

### The AI does the maths

Chinese shopping festivals used to be a maths exam. Spend-over-threshold coupons, cross-store discounts, deposits, balance payments, price-match refunds. Shoppers with postgraduate degrees spent an evening saving 30 yuan and called it leisure.

Now the machine does the sums. In 2025 Tmall rolled out six AI tools for shoppers, including natural-language search and search by photo. Its coupon-allocation algorithm, the company said, handed out 50 billion yuan in vouchers.

In May 2026 Alibaba went further and opened the entire Taobao and Tmall catalogue, more than 4 billion products, to its Qwen AI app. The assistant inside Taobao chooses products in conversation, compares options, stacks coupons, and can track a price for 30 days and place the order automatically once it hits a target. Its first live test was the 618 sale in June.

Note that last feature. The shopper sets a goal, the software waits and buys. It is a sale you no longer need to attend.

### Singles’ Day, then and now

1.  Then: one day. Now: five weeks and counting.
2.  Then: a live sales scoreboard. Now: a count of brands that passed 100 million yuan.
3.  Then: delivery in three days. Now: delivery in thirty minutes.
4.  Then: shoppers do the coupon maths. Now: the AI does.
5.  Then: a festival for single people. Now: a festival where the only one left alone is the shopper, because everyone else is out delivering.

### What it says about the Chinese consumer

First, caution. China’s retail sales rose 2.7% in the first half of 2026, and demand has been propped up by state trade-in subsidies for appliances and other goods. The 2026 budget sets aside 1.3 trillion yuan in ultra-long special treasury bonds, part of it for replacing consumer goods. A shopper who gets discounts from both the store and the state learns to wait for both.

Second, the big date has lost its monopoly. The 618 sale in June, “Double 12” in December and permanent promotions on Douyin livestreams all dilute November’s specialness. A festival that lasts a month stops being a festival and becomes a season.

Third, the competition has shifted from discounts to service. Platforms now compete on delivery speed, search and smart assistants as much as on price. At its 2025 launch, Tmall said it was moving away from “low-price involution” towards “good products at good prices”. Whether that worked will show up in company earnings, not press releases.

For foreign businesses the lesson is simple. Treating Singles’ Day as a record-setting event misses the point. It is better seen as a proving ground: once a year, Chinese retail shows which mechanics it has tested and what it will offer shoppers next.

### The bottom line

At Global Tech Tour we take business leaders to China to see how companies work from the inside, retail and logistics included. In November you learn more in a warehouse or a dark store than from any scoreboard. Not that the scoreboard is on any more.

The 2026 numbers will reach the data firms by mid-November. We do not know them yet. What we do know is that a festival for single people has become the most crowded season of the year, and somehow still a little lonely.
