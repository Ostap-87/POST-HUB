People go to motor shows to look at cars. Sensible, but in Shanghai it does not scale. The 2025 show displayed 1,366 vehicles, 163 of them world premieres. Give each premiere five minutes and you need almost fourteen hours with no lunch, no breaks and no handshakes. Some of the handshakes, by the way, are offered by robots.

So our advice to business delegations sounds odd for a motor show: do not look at the cars. Look at what they are made of, what they are built on and who will sell them. You will see the car on the road soon enough. Its battery supplier you will only meet here.

### Dates and venue: what is confirmed

The 22nd Shanghai International Automobile Industry Exhibition, Auto Shanghai 2027, runs **from 23 April to 2 May 2027** at the National Exhibition and Convention Center (NECC) in Hongqiao. The dates appear on the show’s official website and in an announcement by the Shanghai government.

The schedule:

- **23–24 April**: media days;
- **25–26 April**: trade visitor days;
- **27 April – 2 May**: public days.

The organisers are CCPIT Shanghai and the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers, with Messe München as German co-organiser. The show has been held since 1985 and now takes place every two years, in odd-numbered years; Beijing hosts the big show in even years.

For a business visit, two days out of ten matter: 25 and 26 April. On public days the show becomes a very large and very polite shopping mall where you can talk to an engineer, but only by shouting. Note also that the last days of the show overlap with China’s May Day holiday, when hotels get pricier and offices slow down.

### Scale: a town, not a trade show

According to the organisers, the 2025 show covered 360,000 square metres, with around 1,000 exhibitors from 26 countries and regions and more than a million visits. About 70% of the cars on display were electrified. For 2027, CnEVPost, citing the organisers, reports around 400,000 square metres and a dedicated embodied AI zone for robots and chips.

The venue itself is shaped like a four-leaf clover, with roughly 400,000 square metres of indoor space and less than a mile from the Hongqiao hub that links the airport and the railway station. Convenient. But a clover only brings luck if you know which leaf to walk into.

### Where to go if you are not buying a car

In 2025, eight halls were given to passenger cars and four to technology and the supply chain. The official product list for 2027 includes whole sectors that justify the trip for a specialist.

**Batteries and energy.** Batteries, electric motors and control systems, charging infrastructure, energy storage, the hydrogen chain. Battery makers sit alongside the carmakers: in 2025 [CATL](/en/companies/catl), for example, had a booth in one of the passenger car halls. A good place to compare several suppliers in a day without flying between provinces.

**Electronics and smart systems.** Automotive chips, radar, cameras, connectivity, lighting, infotainment. This is where you see how the Chinese car became a smartphone on wheels, and how many companies build that smartphone for different brands.

**Components.** Drivetrains, chassis, body parts, interiors, standard parts. Few premieres here, plenty of deals.

**Manufacturing.** A separate sector covers production technology, machinery and equipment for car plants, plus measurement, testing, diagnostics and simulation. For engineers this may be the most useful part of the show: not what will drive, but what it will be built on.

A good rule: day one for cars, day two for everything else. By the end of day two, everything else turns out to be more interesting. You still need day one, though. Without it you will not understand why everything else matters.

### Robots at a motor show: salespeople now, assemblers later

The 2025 show had a surprising number of humanoids. According to Yicai, an [AgiBot](/en/companies/agibot) robot worked as a “sales consultant” on the Roewe stand, Unitree robots shook hands with visitors at Changan and FAW, Chery showed its robot Mornine and [Xpeng](/en/companies/xpeng) its humanoid Iron. Xpeng’s CEO said at the time that the company had begun training robots to work in factories.

How should a business visitor read this? A robot on a stand is, first of all, advertising. A robot shaking hands demonstrates that it can shake hands. The question to ask: where does this robot work apart from the stand, how many hours per shift, and who maintains it? If the answer is “it’s a concept”, it is a concept. Nothing wrong with that. Just do not buy it.

For 2027 the organisers have announced a dedicated zone for robots and smart hardware. There will probably be more humanoids. One hopes not only at reception.

### Six questions for a supplier’s booth

1.  Who are your series production customers, and since when? (Customers, not “partners”.)
2.  Where is this component made, and how many plants do you have?
3.  Which export certifications do you hold, and for which markets?
4.  What are the minimum order quantity and lead times?
5.  Can we visit the plant after the show?
6.  Which of the things on display are in series production, and which are for the next show?

Question six matters most. At a motor show everything looks finished. Even the things that will be finished in 2029.

### Combining the show with factory visits

Shanghai is a convenient starting point: the Yangtze River Delta is densely packed with EV, battery and component plants, and high-speed rail puts neighbouring cities closer than some parts of Shanghai itself. We covered an automotive cluster route in our article on an expedition from Hefei to Shanghai.

A plan that works: 23–24 April for arrival and preparation, 25–26 April at the show on trade days, then three or four days visiting the companies you shortlisted at their booths. Aim to fly home before the May Day holiday.

Global Tech Tour runs business expeditions to China: closed visits to companies across 17 industries, ready-made and bespoke programmes. A trade show can be the starting point of such a programme, with company visits as the follow-up. The final list of visits is always confirmed by the hosts.

### The bottom line

The Shanghai show is a shop window the whole world watches. But every shop window has a back room, and the back room is often more interesting. That is where the batteries, chips, sensors and machine tools live: everything without which a premiere would have stayed a drawing.

Premieres come by the hundred. Suppliers by the thousand. And the one you need, as usual, is just one.
