Every large country has a place where the rules are a little softer. Usually it is a resort, sometimes a special economic zone, and occasionally the boss’s country house.

In China it is the island of Hainan, and on it a small patch of land near the city of Qionghai called Boao Lecheng. Here you can do something the rest of the country cannot yet: use drugs and medical devices approved abroad but not registered in China.

A caveat: this is not about treatment or where to go for medicine. It is about how the state built a regulatory sandbox for healthcare, and what that sandbox offers manufacturers, hospitals and investors.

### A zone where the drug arrives before the registration

The Boao Lecheng International Medical Tourism Pilot Zone was established in 2013 with State Council approval. The logic is simple. Registering a new drug takes years in any country. Meanwhile, patients who need it either wait or travel abroad. The zone offers a third way: a drug approved abroad can be imported into Lecheng under special permission and used in the zone’s hospitals.

The limit matters: all of this happens only inside the zone. The patient comes to Lecheng, is treated in a local clinic and leaves. The drug is not sold on the mainland until it passes normal registration.

A sandbox is not a hole in the fence. It is a fenced-off playground with its own guard.

According to the National Development and Reform Commission (December 2025), more than 520 international innovative drugs and devices had their first use in China in Lecheng, with more than 190,000 related patient [visits](/en/expeditions). By June 2026, Science and Technology Daily was reporting more than 560 such products and partnerships with over 180 manufacturers from more than 20 countries. In 2024 the zone received 413,700 medical tourists, up 36.8% on the year before.

One detail the zone likes to mention: before it existed, imported innovative drugs and devices in China often cost 1.5 to 2 times international prices. Imports into the zone now carry zero tariffs.

### Real-world data: the zone’s real value

If Lecheng were only a place to get a foreign drug, it would interest patients. Manufacturers care for a different reason.

Since 2019 the zone has run a pilot on using real-world clinical data for registration in China. A drug is used in Lecheng hospitals, outcome data are collected under set rules, and those data can be taken into account by the regulator when reviewing a national registration application. That can shorten or simplify some of the clinical work that would otherwise have to be repeated in China.

The first approvals came quickly: by October 2021, according to Chinese press reports, three products had reached the market this way. The programme has expanded since.

For an international manufacturer, this changes the calculation. Entering China used to start with a long wait. Now you can begin using the product in the zone, collect data and prepare registration in parallel. The zone becomes not just a treatment site but a front door to a huge market.

An ordinary queue moves slowly. Lecheng is a queue with data, and it moves faster.

### 18 December 2025: the island on a special regime

Now the wider context. On 18 December 2025, the whole of Hainan Island moved to a special customs regime, summed up in two phrases: “freer access at the first line” and “regulated access at the second line”.

The first line is the border between Hainan and the rest of the world. Here the regime became much more relaxed: the share of goods eligible for zero tariffs on entry to the island rose from 21% to 74%, and the list of duty-free tariff lines grew from about 1,900 to more than 6,600.

The second line is the border between Hainan and mainland China, where normal customs control applies. There is one important exception: if a good imported duty-free into Hainan is processed on the island and gains at least 30% in value, it can be sold on the mainland free of import duty.

The first results came out after a month. According to official figures, 21,000 new companies registered on the island in the regime’s first month, up 16.4% year on year. Zero-tariff imports through the first line reached 753 million yuan, up 38.9%. Processed goods sold to the mainland under the value-added rule came to about 86 million yuan. Duty-free shop sales were 4.86 billion yuan, up 46.8%. Fuller results are published by the authorities as statistics accumulate, and are worth tracking through the provincial government and customs.

The island has moved closer to the world and slightly further from the mainland. The geography is the same. The customs desk has moved.

### What it means for healthcare

For the healthcare industry, the island’s regime and the Lecheng zone fit together. The zone provides a regulatory exception: you can use what is not yet registered. The island provides a customs one: you can import at zero tariff and, with enough local processing, sell on the mainland.

In practice that opens several scenarios. An international manufacturer can use Lecheng for first use and data collection. A company wanting to produce for the Chinese market can consider Hainan for processing and assembly. Service providers, from clinical research to cold-chain logistics, find demand that did not exist before.

An important caveat: the details for any specific product depend on separate rules and permits, and the general logic of the regime is no substitute for advice from lawyers and the regulator.

### A short glossary for a trip to Hainan

First line: the border that is easy to cross on the way in. Second line: the border that is hard to cross on the way out. Special permission: the document people come to Lecheng for. Real-world data: what stays behind after the patient leaves. Zero tariff: the island’s favourite number. Thirty percent: the line between “imported” and “made here”.

### The bottom line

Hainan is often seen as a resort of palm trees and duty-free shops. True, but not the whole truth. Behind the resort is one of China’s most interesting regulatory experiments: an island that allows itself a little more than the mainland and tests what is worth rolling out nationwide.

For medical companies studying the Chinese market, Lecheng is worth seeing in person. Not because it is the world’s largest medical zone, but because this is where you can watch the Chinese regulator try something new: on a limited territory first, under supervision, with data. And only then everywhere.

Good ideas in China are said to start as experiments. Hainan is a whole laboratory. With a beach.
