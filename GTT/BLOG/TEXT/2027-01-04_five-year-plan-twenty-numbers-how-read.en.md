Five-year plans have an image problem in the West. They summon pictures of tractor statistics, heroic posters and targets that were always, somehow, overfulfilled. So when China adopts a new one, many readers smile. Smile by all means, but read it too. A Chinese plan is not a slogan on a factory wall. It is a fairly precise map of where the state will steer money, credit and official attention over the next five years.

The 15th plan covers 2026 to 2030. We have read the published documents and will summarise them the way we would for an [expedition](/en/expeditions) participant on the flight over.

### How a plan is made

A Chinese plan arrives in two steps. First, in October 2025, the Fourth Plenum of the Communist Party’s Central Committee adopted “recommendations” for the plan: the framework and general priorities. Then the government prepares the “Outline”, the detailed document with numbers. The draft went to the National People’s Congress on 5 March 2026, was approved on 12 March, and Xinhua published the full text the next day.

It is long: 18 parts, 62 chapters, 171 sections. You do not need to read it all. You need to know where to look.

A plan is not a promise. It is a list of things officials will be held to account for. Officials, note, not you.

### Twenty numbers worth knowing

At the heart of the document sits a table of 20 headline indicators for 2030, in five groups. Some are “expected”, meaning guidance. Others are “binding”, and authorities at every level answer for them.

Economy: three indicators. There is no five-year GDP growth number. Growth is to stay “within a reasonable range”, with annual targets set separately. The longer-term aim remains: by 2035, per-capita GDP should reach the level of a moderately developed country.

Innovation: three indicators. R&D spending is to grow by more than 7% a year. High-value invention patents should exceed 22 per 10,000 people. The core digital economy’s share of GDP is to rise from about 10.2% to 12.5%.

Wellbeing: seven indicators, including average schooling of 11.7 years for the working-age population (a binding target), life expectancy of 80, urban unemployment below 5.5%, more doctors and nurses per thousand people, and more nursing-care places in elderly care.

Green development: five binding indicators. Cut carbon intensity of GDP by 17% over five years, raise non-fossil energy to 25% of consumption, bring average PM2.5 in cities below 27 µg/m³, raise forest cover to 25.8% and the share of good-quality surface water to 85%.

Security: two binding indicators. Grain production capacity of 725 billion kg and energy production capacity of 5.8 billion tonnes of standard coal equivalent.

Notice the balance. GDP growth, as in the previous plan, remains a line without a number, while carbon, grain and energy have become lines with binding numbers. That may be the shortest possible summary of the era’s priorities.

### The industries on the list

For business, the plan’s real value lies in its lists of industries. There are two.

The first is strategic emerging industries, meant to carry the economy over the next few years: next-generation IT, new energy, new materials, intelligent connected electric vehicles, robotics, biomedicine, high-end equipment, and aerospace.

The second is future industries, bets with a horizon of ten years or more: quantum technology, biomanufacturing, hydrogen and fusion energy, brain-computer interfaces, embodied AI and 6G.

Separately, the plan commits to a full-scale “AI+” programme: bringing artificial intelligence into every sector, backed by more computing power, better algorithms and more data.

If your industry is on one of these lists, your Chinese partners will have easier access to money, land and official attention for the next five years. If it is not, that is no death sentence, but explaining their strategy to the bank will be harder.

### Year one: the 2026 targets

The five-year plan sets the direction; the annual government work report sets the speed. For 2026 the GDP growth target is 4.5–5% and the inflation target around 2%. The budget deficit stays at 4% of GDP. Ultra-long special treasury bonds of 1.3 trillion yuan are earmarked for strategic projects, equipment upgrades and consumer trade-ins, and local governments may issue 4.4 trillion yuan in special bonds, part of it to clear arrears to suppliers.

The economy grew 4.7% in the first half: 5% in the first quarter and 4.3% in the second. That is the picture as of October 2026; full-year results come later.

### Five rules for reading a Chinese plan

1.  Read the verbs. “Develop”, “support” and “promote” are not the same. The strongest verb usually sits next to whatever will get the most money.
2.  Look for what has gone. Targets that became awkward quietly disappear, and that is information too.
3.  Watch the binding indicators. Expected numbers get discussed; binding ones get reported on.
4.  Do not confuse a plan with a forecast. A plan says what is wanted, not what will happen.
5.  Do not read the whole thing over a weekend. By Monday you will be saying “in the context of implementation” and unable to stop.

### Opening up: what foreigners are promised

The section on external economic ties uses familiar language: expand high-standard opening up, deepen trade and investment cooperation, advance “institutional opening”, meaning closer alignment with international rules. The concrete items include hubs for China–Europe (and Asia) freight trains in 14 cities, closer alignment between Chinese and international standards, and mutual recognition of energy efficiency, water efficiency and carbon footprint standards for key products.

That last point matters if you buy from China or sell into it. A product’s carbon footprint is gradually becoming a parameter alongside price and lead time.

### How to use it in business

First, check your industry against the lists. If you source robotics or new energy equipment from China, your suppliers will be growing and investing. If you are in a sector the plan describes as “traditional, to be upgraded”, expect consolidation and automation.

Second, look at the regions. Provinces and big cities write their own plans under the national one, with specific projects and sites. That is where you can see where new factories will go.

Third, check on the ground. You can read the document at home, but seeing how it turns into workshops, laboratories and logistics hubs is best done in person. Global Tech Tour runs business expeditions to China with closed visits to companies across 17 industries, many of them straight out of this plan’s lists.

### The bottom line

Five-year plans used to be famous for being overfulfilled on paper. China’s are better known for being read closely: by businesses, banks, local governments and foreign competitors alike.

Five years is not long. It is five new versions of the same smartphone and, with luck, one new factory. In five years we will open the document again and see what came true. We promise not to smile in advance.
