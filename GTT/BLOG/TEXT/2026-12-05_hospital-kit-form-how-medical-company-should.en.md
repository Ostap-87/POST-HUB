Picture a hospital taken apart and laid out across exhibition halls. Scanners in one hall, reagents in another, operating theatres in a third, and in a fourth, beds that can do more than some patients. In the middle stands a person with a “buyer” badge, trying to work out where to start.

That is CMEF, the China International Medical Equipment Fair. You cannot reassemble the hospital in four days. But you can find out what Chinese hospitals are built from, and what it costs.

### What the fair is

CMEF is organised by Reed Sinopharm Exhibitions (listed by JETRO as RX Sinopharm). It has run for more than four decades and takes place twice a year, in spring and autumn.

The spring edition is the main one. In 2026 it ran 9–12 April at the National Exhibition and Convention Center in Shanghai. According to JETRO, it had around 5,000 exhibitors on roughly 350,000 square metres, and the previous edition drew 300,000 visitors, more than 10,000 of them from abroad. The organiser’s closing release put spring 2026 attendance at over 300,000 participants from more than 150 countries and regions.

The autumn edition moves around the country. In 2025 it was held in Guangzhou; the autumn 2026 edition was scheduled for 21–24 October in Beijing. It is smaller, but can be more convenient if you work with a particular Chinese region.

**At the time of writing, CMEF 2027 dates have not been officially announced. Please check with the organiser.** The spring show is traditionally held in Shanghai in April, but planning a trip by tradition is like diagnosing from a photograph.

### Why buyers come from all over the world

China is a major producer of medical equipment, and much of the industry gathers at CMEF. You will find large corporations and hundreds of mid-sized firms that make one product and make it well.

For a foreign buyer, the second group matters most. You can find a big manufacturer without a trade show. The company that makes exactly the patient monitor or dental unit you need, but spends nothing on marketing abroad, you will only find at its booth.

There is also a business programme. According to the organiser, spring 2026 featured more than 100 forums on technology, clinical practice, regulation and business. The international WeTalk stage hosted 19 sessions focused on markets including Russia, Brazil and ASEAN, and the WeTour programme ran about ten guided tours for consulates, hospitals and distributors.

### Sections: where to go

CMEF’s product range is wide, and that is the main danger. Walk the halls in order and by day three you will know more about wheelchairs than about whatever you came for.

The main areas the organiser lists:

- **medical imaging**: CT, MRI, ultrasound, X-ray;
- **diagnostics**: in vitro, molecular, point-of-care;
- **electromedical and optical devices**, patient monitoring;
- **smart healthcare, wearables and AI**;
- **hospital construction and operating rooms**;
- **rehabilitation** and elderly care;
- **ambulance equipment**.

The new technology zone deserves special attention. In spring 2026 it featured wide-body high-definition CT scanners, surgical robots and an invasive brain-computer interface its developer described as the world’s first. At CMEF, “world’s first” is heard about as often as “good morning”. Both deserve the same response: polite, with follow-up questions.

### How a medical company should prepare

**Three to four months out.** Decide exactly what you are looking for: a product, a component supplier, a localisation partner or a technology. A medical company that goes to CMEF “to look at trends” comes home with trends. One that goes for a specific device comes home with three offers and the question of which to choose.

**Two months out.** Register online: the fair is open to trade visitors only. Go through the exhibitor list on the official website and build a shortlist of twenty or thirty companies instead of three thousand. If you plan negotiations, book meetings in advance: strong companies have full diaries during the show.

**One month out.** Prepare what you will need for a supply conversation: your market’s requirements for registering medical devices, the certificates you need, technical specifications. Medical device registration in most markets is a separate and slow process, so find out early whether a supplier is willing to support it.

**Who should go.** Three people work best: a decision-maker, an engineer or clinical specialist, and whoever handles registration and procurement. Without the engineer, you buy something beautiful. Without the regulatory specialist, you buy something you cannot sell.

### Five questions for a manufacturer at the booth

1.  Which registrations does this device already have, and in which countries?
2.  Is it made in your own plant or by a contract manufacturer?
3.  Who will train staff and service the equipment in our country?
4.  Which spare parts and consumables are needed, and for how many years do you guarantee supply?
5.  Can we visit your plant after the show?

Question five is the litmus test. If they are keen to invite you to the factory, there probably is a factory.

A trade show shows who makes what. To see how they make it, you need to go to the plant. We covered how to see a manufacturer, a hospital and a distributor in one trip in our article on a medtech [expedition](/en/expeditions) to China.

A sensible plan: the fair at the start of the trip, then two or three days of visits to companies on your shortlist. ### The bottom line

Medicine is an industry where mistakes cost more than anywhere else. So treat the fair like a check-up: system by system, prepared in advance, no self-diagnosis.

You will not assemble a hospital at CMEF. But you may find the people who will assemble it with you. Honestly, that is half the job.
