Every driver knows the old line: a car is four wheels and an infinite number of things to worry about. Electric cars added a fifth, very modern one: will I make it to the next charger?

China’s [NIO](/en/companies/nio) answered it the way a nursery teacher handles a broken toy. Don’t fix it, swap it. You drive into a station, a robot removes the depleted battery and fits a charged one, and you stay in your seat long enough to read one message, if you’re lucky.

Eight years ago this looked naive. Today it looks like infrastructure.

### From one station to four thousand

NIO opened its first swap station in Shenzhen in May 2018. The first million swaps took 29 months.

On 6 February 2026 the company celebrated its 100 millionth swap. It then had 3,729 swap stations in China, 1,020 of them on highways. On 7 August 2026 the counter passed 120 million and the 4,000th station opened. According to CnEVPost, there were 4,006 stations at that point, 1,049 on highways, connecting 550 cities. The latest 20 million swaps took about six months.

NIO added about 680 stations a year in 2024 and 2025, and targeted more than 1,000 in 2026, increasingly through partners: a June 2026 agreement with Anhui-based Zhongan Energy covers 500 stations within a year.

The company also says 80.1% of its users have a station within 3 km of home or work, and that 6.35 billion kWh has passed through the network. These are company figures, but the order of magnitude is clear.

Advertisements like to say “a swap in three minutes”. The exact time depends on the station generation and the car, so let’s put it carefully: minutes, not the half hour of a fast charger, let alone a night on a home socket.

### The model: you don’t own the battery, and that’s the point

The second half of the idea matters more. NIO lets customers buy the car without the battery and subscribe to the battery instead. It’s called BaaS, Battery as a Service. The car costs noticeably less, and the battery is a monthly fee. Prices and terms have changed over time, so check them at the date of purchase.

For the driver, battery ageing stops being your problem: the station fits a pack that has passed inspection. You can temporarily take a bigger battery for a long trip, which NIO calls a “flexible upgrade” and counts in the hundreds of thousands.

For the company, the battery turns from a car part into an asset managed by the network operator. It can be charged when power is cheap, tested, repaired and moved between stations. A swap station is really a small energy warehouse with a robot at the door.

A line for your CFO: if the battery is a third of the car’s cost, not selling it is another way of selling the car.

### Partners: who else wants to swap

The key question for any swap network is how many brands use it. One brand makes it a perk. Many brands make it infrastructure.

At first, the network served only NIO cars. Today it serves the group’s three brands: NIO, Onvo and Firefly. In 2023–2024 NIO signed swap partnerships with Changan, [Geely](/en/companies/geely), Chery, GAC and JAC, though details on most were thin.

In March 2025, NIO and [CATL](/en/companies/catl) announced a strategic partnership: CATL would invest up to 2.5 billion yuan in NIO Power, and its Choco-swap standard would be used in future Firefly models. According to CnEVPost, that investment never materialised. A useful reminder that an announced deal and a closed deal are different animals, even in fast-moving China.

The latest development came on 28 September 2026. NIO signed a definitive agreement with Geely Holding: Geely gets 30% of NIO Power in exchange for its commercial-fleet swap business, Yiyi Power, plus 640 million yuan in cash. NIO Power’s post-money valuation is about 16 billion yuan, and NIO keeps control with about 63.6%. Geely can invest another 640 million to reach 34%, and its stake is tied to operating milestones: if they are missed, it can be cut, but not below 20%. NIO, in turn, takes 10% of Geely’s charging company, Haohan Energy. NIO Power’s target is 10,000 stations by 2030.

From the outside, the meaning fits in one sentence: competitors decided the network was cheaper to share than to duplicate.

### Where the model hits limits

**Standards.** A swappable battery has to match in size, mounting and connectors, and every carmaker has its own platforms. Without a single national standard for passenger cars, each partnership is a separate engineering project. That’s why CATL pushes its standard and NIO pushes its own.

**Money.** A swap station costs more than a charger: it stores several expensive packs and runs a robot. The network pays off when stations are busy, which explains the hunt for partners and the Geely deal.

**NIO itself.** The company lost money for years. Q2 2026 looked different: deliveries rose 49% year on year to 107,658 vehicles, revenue grew 69% to 32.1 billion yuan, and gross margin reached 18.4% versus 10% a year earlier. The GAAP net loss narrowed to 528 million yuan from almost 5 billion, and adjusted net profit turned positive at about 26 million yuan. Not yet durable profitability, but no longer the picture behind the gloomy forecasts of earlier years.

### Three questions to ask at a swap station

1.  How many swaps does this station do per day, and how many batteries are stored inside?
2.  How many car brands does it serve today, and how many will it serve next year?
3.  Who owns the battery that was just fitted to the car?

In China, the third question now has three correct answers, all legally different.

### What it means beyond China

A swap network is not only about electric cars. It shows how an infrastructure business can grow inside a manufacturer, get spun out into its own company and then open up to competitors. The same logic appears in logistics, warehousing and charging for commercial fleets.

At Global Tech Tour we run business expeditions to China, and automotive and energy are among the 17 industries where we arrange closed company visits. A swap station is a small site that explains a big idea in five minutes: a service can matter more than a product when the product ages and the service doesn’t.

They say the secret of a long marriage is never changing partners. NIO decided the secret of electric driving is changing batteries. So far, that relationship has outlasted most of the sceptics’ predictions.
