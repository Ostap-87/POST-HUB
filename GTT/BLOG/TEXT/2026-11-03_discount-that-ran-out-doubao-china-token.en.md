Every procurement manager knows that the most dangerous discount is the one you get used to. While it lasts, budgets, processes and the board’s expectations are rebuilt around it. Then a letter arrives citing “changing market conditions” and politely puts the price back where it was. The budget, however, stays where it went.

China’s AI market went through that entire cycle in two years. The leading role belonged to [ByteDance](/en/companies/bytedance) and its Doubao models, and for an executive it may be the most instructive story in Chinese AI. Not because everything there is cheap, but because you can watch the price move.

### How it started

ByteDance, the company behind TikTok and Douyin, opened its Doubao chatbot to the public in August 2023. In May 2024 its cloud arm, Volcano Engine, launched a family of Doubao models for business and priced the main one at 0.0008 yuan per thousand tokens, 99.3% below the industry average according to the company.

The market was already moving, and the rest took only days. On 6 May DeepSeek had released V2 at one yuan per million input tokens. On 11 May Zhipu cut its price fivefold. Doubao followed on 15 May. On 21 May [Alibaba](/en/companies/alibaba) cut Qwen-Long by 97%, and Baidu made two Ernie models free the same day. The next day [Tencent](/en/companies/tencent) and iFlytek opened free access to their lightweight models.

In two weeks the price of machine-generated text in China fell by orders of magnitude, in places to zero. In retail this is called a sale. In cloud computing it is called a market-capture strategy. The difference is that a retail sale ends on Sunday.

### What the discount bought

It bought a habit. In May 2024 Doubao models processed around 120 billion text tokens a day. In March 2026 Volcano Engine reported 120 trillion a day, a thousandfold increase, with volume doubling in the previous three months alone. The number of companies that had used more than a trillion tokens on the platform rose from 100 at the end of 2025 to 140.

The consumer side is just as striking. QuestMobile counted 382 million monthly active users for the Doubao app in May 2026, against 167 million for Alibaba’s Qwen and 130 million for DeepSeek. All AI apps in China together: 499 million.

As intended, the discount turned an experiment into a habit. And habits are not cancelled by memo.

### How the war ended

In 2026 the pendulum swung back. On 11 March Tencent Cloud ended free trials of several models and raised prices on two Hunyuan 2.0 models more than fivefold. On 18 March Alibaba Cloud announced price rises of 5–34% on AI computing products from 18 April, citing global demand, supply-chain costs and rising token consumption. Zhipu raised prices by 30% in February and by another 8–17% in April. In May ByteDance began testing paid Doubao subscriptions at 68, 200 and 500 yuan a month for heavy tasks, keeping a free tier.

Prices went up and demand did not leave. That is the real outcome: the price war did not ruin the market, it trained it. Now it pays.

### Who won? Depends what you count

Here the Chinese market offers a lesson for anyone who reads industry rankings.

According to IDC data published in May 2026, Volcano Engine ranks first in China’s public-cloud model-as-a-service market with a 49.5% share. That is a measure of usage.

According to Omdia, Alibaba Cloud led China’s AI cloud market in 2025 (56.7 billion yuan) with 38.1%, followed by Volcano Engine at 20.4%, Baidu Cloud at 9.4% and Tencent Cloud at 6.3%. That is a measure of revenue.

Both are right. One counts tokens, the other counts money. One leader sells the most machine text; the other earns the most from the infrastructure around it. When a Chinese partner tells you they are number one, the first question is: by which count?

### The other players

Each big player has its own logic. Alibaba bets on open models and the cloud. Baidu, whose CEO said in spring 2024 that open models made little sense, open-sourced its own Ernie 4.5 family in June 2025. Tencent works through its products: in Q1 2026 it spent 31.9 billion yuan on capital expenditure, open-sourced Hy3 Preview in April, and saw advertising revenue grow almost 20% on the back of an upgraded AI recommendation model. According to 36Kr, WeChat is building its own model and plans to plug mini programs into it as “skills” for AI agents. Tencent also promoted its Yuanbao assistant generously, handing out roughly a billion yuan in red envelopes through it at Chinese New Year 2026.

Five big players, five strategies, and none of them is simply “sell the model”.

### Five takeaways for AI buyers

1.  The price of a token is a variable. Model future increases, not just today’s rate.
2.  Avoid locking into one supplier so tightly that switching costs more than a price rise.
3.  Look at the cost of the whole task, not a single token: models of different sizes do the same job for different money.
4.  Free access is a stage, not a tariff. The 2024–2026 record shows it.
5.  Check the methodology behind market rankings: share by tokens and share by revenue produce different leaders.

### What to look at in China

ByteDance and Volcano Engine are based in Beijing, Alibaba in Hangzhou, Tencent in Shenzhen and Baidu in Beijing again. The price war is best observed not at the combatants’ offices but at their customers’: manufacturers, retailers, logistics and service companies that have switched AI suppliers once or twice in two years and can tell you what it was like.

The questions are simple. Whose models do you use, and why those? How did your AI spending change in 2026 compared with 2025? What did it cost to switch suppliers, if you did? What would you do differently if you were starting now?

### The bottom line

ByteDance made AI a mass product the same way it once made short video one: first let everyone try it, then start counting. The discount has run out. The habit has stayed.

The best discount is the one that leaves you with a skill as well as an invoice. All the others simply expire.
