“China plus one” sounds like a diversification strategy: don’t keep all your eggs in one basket. In practice, something curious happens. There are indeed two baskets now. But the same hen is still laying the eggs for both.

Numbers first, then the map.

### Two figures worth putting side by side

In 2025 Vietnam exported $153 billion of goods to the United States, up from $119.5 billion a year earlier. Its trade surplus with the US was nearly $134 billion by Vietnamese figures.

In the same year Vietnam imported $186 billion of goods from China, up from $144.2 billion.

Exports to the US rose by $33 billion. Imports from China rose by $42 billion. That is neither a coincidence nor a conspiracy. It is the model: components, materials and equipment come from China, are assembled in Vietnam and leave with Vietnamese origin.

“Plus one” turned out to be less a replacement for China than its satellite workshop.

### Investment: who is arriving

Vietnam’s statistics office says the country attracted $38.4 billion in foreign investment in 2025, roughly flat on the year. Disbursements reached $27.6 billion, up 9% and a five-year high. There were 4,054 new projects, 20% more than in 2024.

By newly registered capital Singapore ranked first ($4.84 billion, almost 28%) and China second ($3.64 billion, 21%). The Singapore line is often Chinese too, since many mainland companies invest through Singapore entities. We cover that in a separate article.

More than half of new capital (56.5%) went into manufacturing. Vietnam is not selling beaches. It is selling factory floors.

### Bac Ninh: where you can see it

If China plus one looks like a landscape anywhere, it is in Bac Ninh province, an hour from Hanoi. [Samsung](/en/companies/samsung)’s suppliers started building here in the late 2000s, followed by Apple’s. In 2024 Bac Ninh led Vietnam in foreign investment with $5.12 billion, and in the first eight months of 2025 it led again with $4.68 billion, ahead of Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi.

Since July 2025, after an administrative reform, neighbouring Bac Giang has been part of Bac Ninh, bringing [Foxconn](/en/companies/foxconn) and Luxshare projects with it. China’s Goertek, which makes earbuds for Apple, has built four plants in the province since 2013, with registered capital above $1.3 billion. Luxshare received a licence for a $300 million smartphone project.

Caixin describes three waves of Chinese arrivals in Bac Ninh: after the 2018 US tariff increases, after the pandemic, and from late 2024. Each wave answered an event that happened somewhere other than Vietnam.

### The other side

Factory density quickly becomes a fight for workers. Big manufacturers poach from each other and wages rise; Caixin reports Goertek job ads offering assemblers up to 3,800 yuan a month. Industrial land is getting pricier too.

Vietnam is still cheaper than coastal China, but the gap is narrowing. Only the first factory in a province gets it cheap. The tenth pays market rates.

### Tariffs: rules that change faster than a plant gets built

In July 2025 the US and Vietnam announced a framework: a 20% tariff on most Vietnamese goods and 40% on goods “transshipped” through Vietnam from third countries. What counts as transshipment was not defined in detail.

In February 2026 the US Supreme Court struck down tariffs imposed under emergency economic powers legislation. The administration turned to other tools, and in March 2026 opened Section 301 investigations into many economies, Vietnam among them.

We will not guess which rate applies by the time you read this. What we will say is that rules of origin are becoming the central question. A Vietnamese plant that merely repacks Chinese goods offers less and less protection. A plant that genuinely adds value offers more and more.

### How to tell “plus one” from “plus a signboard”

1.  Ask what share of the product’s value is created in Vietnam. If the answer is “that is a complicated question”, note it down.
2.  Ask where the key component is made. If it is Shenzhen, and Vietnam supplies screws and boxes, that is assembly, not manufacturing.
3.  Ask how many engineers work on site rather than “fly in when needed”.
4.  Ask when the plant was built. A factory opened six months before a tariff deadline was not built for you.

### What it means for buyers sourcing from China

For most buyers, the Vietnam story matters indirectly. Most purchasing still happens in China. But your supplier may move part of its capacity in the next few years, and that will affect lead times, prices, quality control and who exactly is responsible for your order.

So it is worth asking a Chinese manufacturer a question that used to go unasked: where will your plant be in three years? The answer tells you a lot about how the company sees risk and which markets it is building for.

One more practical point. China–Vietnam supply chains do not make China less important. To understand Bac Ninh you need to understand Shenzhen and Dongguan, where products are designed, key components are made and decisions about which workshop moves where are taken.

### The bottom line

Every few years global business announces it is leaving China. Then it counts how many components it needs to buy in China to do so.

Northern Vietnam is growing fast and for real. But its growth shows less an escape from China than how far China can now reach.

Plus one. Sometimes it is just another address for the same family.
