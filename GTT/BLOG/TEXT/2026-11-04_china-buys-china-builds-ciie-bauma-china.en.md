November in Shanghai is the month the city plays two roles at once. Early in the month, China buys: at the China International Import Expo, foreign companies show their goods to Chinese buyers. Late in the month, every other year, China builds: at bauma China, excavators, cranes and concrete pumps stand so close together that the show itself looks like a construction site.

Doing both in one trip is an obvious idea, and we like it too. But before buying tickets, look at the calendar. The two shows run on different schedules, and they line up less often than you would hope.

### CIIE: the show where the seller is foreign

The China International Import Expo has been held in Shanghai every year since 2018. Its format is unusual: exhibitors are companies registered outside China’s customs territory, and visitors are Chinese buyers, distributors and investors. China is not selling here. It is choosing what to buy.

The scale matches. According to the Shanghai government, the eighth expo in 2025 brought together about 4,100 foreign companies, including 290 Fortune 500 companies and industry leaders, and recorded a record 83.49 billion dollars in intended deals. Total exhibition space exceeded 430,000 square metres.

The business exhibition is divided into themed areas:

- food and agricultural products;
- automobiles and automotive technology;
- technology equipment: automation, robotics, chips, AI, energy;
- consumer goods;
- medical equipment and healthcare products;
- trade in services: logistics, consulting, finance;
- a special section for early-stage innovative companies.

The ninth expo was scheduled for 5–10 November 2026 at the National Exhibition and Convention Center in Hongqiao. **At the time of writing, dates for the tenth CIIE in 2027 have not been announced. Please check with the organiser.**

Who should go? Companies that want to sell into China and see how other foreigners do it: how they design stands, which language they use with buyers, whom they bring to negotiations. It is a rare chance to see the Chinese market through the eyes of those trying to enter it. And anyone working with Chinese distributors: they come to CIIE looking for new brands.

### bauma China: a building site under a roof

bauma China is the Chinese edition of Munich’s bauma, one of the world’s largest construction machinery shows. It is organised by Messe München, has been held in Shanghai since 2002, and takes place every two years.

The organiser’s figures for 2024: 3,542 exhibitors from 32 countries and regions, 281,488 visitors from 188 countries and regions, 330,000 square metres.

In 2026 the show uses two venues for the first time: **23–26 November** at the Shanghai World Expo Exhibition & Convention Center (future technologies, forums, demonstrations) and **24–27 November** at the Shanghai New International Expo Centre (traditional machinery, vehicles, components). According to the Shanghai government, total space is a record 400,000 square metres, with around 4,000 exhibitors expected. One pass covers both venues, with free shuttle buses between them.

What should a business visitor look at? Not just the machines. The show is the best place to see how Chinese construction equipment is changing from the inside: electric and hybrid machines, telematics, remote operation, autonomous modes. An excavator that knows where to dig is still mostly a demo. An excavator that tells you it needs an oil change is already a product.

### Why “one trip” does not always work

Now for the calendar, which is the real reason for this article.

**First, bauma China is held every two years, in even-numbered years.** There will be no bauma China in Shanghai in 2027. CIIE, meanwhile, is annual. So the November “import plus construction” combination is only available in even years, and the next one after 2026, if the cycle holds, falls in 2028. Its dates, naturally, should be checked with the organiser.

**Second, even in an even year there can be a gap.** In 2026, CIIE was scheduled for 5–10 November and bauma China for 23–27 November. Nearly two weeks apart. You can wait in Shanghai, but that is an expensive wait. You can fly home and come back, but that is two trips.

**Third, the shows sit at opposite ends of the city.** CIIE is in Hongqiao, in the west, next to the airport and railway station. Both bauma China venues are in Pudong, in the east. Staying “in the middle” in Shanghai means being equally far from everything.

Two shows in one city in one month do not make one trip. It is like two birthdays in the same family: close together, but you still need two presents.

### Turning the gap into a programme

Two weeks between shows is not a problem if you fill them with work. China’s construction machinery industry is concentrated in a few clusters, all reachable from Shanghai within a day.

The best known is Changsha, capital of Hunan province, home to the headquarters of [Sany](/en/companies/sany) and Zoomlion. According to the provincial government, in early 2024 the city led all Chinese cities in construction machinery exports. Another centre is Xuzhou in Jiangsu province, home of [XCMG](/en/companies/xcmg), whose name literally stands for Xuzhou Construction Machinery Group. We wrote separately about how Sany digitised its factories.

A plan that can work in an even year:

1.  **Early November**: CIIE in Hongqiao, especially the technology equipment and automotive areas.
2.  **Mid-month**: company visits to makers of construction equipment, components and hydraulics.
3.  **Late November**: bauma China. After the factories, you will see the stands with very different eyes.

In an odd year, November in Shanghai means CIIE plus factories. Not bad at all. Just no excavators indoors.

### Four ways to ruin November in Shanghai

1.  Booking flights without checking whether bauma China is on this year.
2.  Staying in Hongqiao during bauma, or in Pudong during CIIE.
3.  Expecting to find Chinese manufacturers at an import expo. The exhibitors are foreign.
4.  Leaving your warm jacket at home. Shanghai’s November is mild only in photographs.

### The bottom line

November in Shanghai shows China from both sides of the counter. In Hongqiao it is a buyer, demanding and curious. In Pudong it is a maker, confident and loud, like a building site.

You cannot see both sides in one trip every year. But even one side, seen with your own eyes, explains more than both of them read about at home.
