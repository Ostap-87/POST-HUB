Not long ago, the mental map of automotive China had a handful of pins: Shanghai, Guangzhou, Changchun, Chongqing somewhere to the west. Hefei was not on it. Hefei was in the atlas, in small print.

In 2020, Anhui province, whose capital is Hefei, built 1.16 million vehicles and ranked eighth in China. In 2025 it built 3.69 million, including 1.79 million new energy vehicles, and ranked first on both counts.

Most regions take a generation to do that. Anhui took five years. That is why we go: to see it being done while it is still being done.

### Why start in Hefei

The story told at every investment forum in China goes like this. In 2020, [NIO](/en/companies/nio) was in serious trouble. Hefei’s state-owned investors put 7 billion yuan into the company, gained its headquarters and production, and in effect launched the city’s EV industry. [BYD](/en/companies/byd) and Volkswagen Anhui followed.

Our favourite number: BYD’s site in Changfeng County, about 40 km from central Hefei, went from first talks to signing in 23 days, from signing to groundbreaking in 42 days, and from groundbreaking to the first car off the line in ten months. In many places, that is roughly how long it takes to approve the fence.

In 2024 Hefei produced 1.376 million NEVs, second only to Shenzhen. Local media report that in 2025 it moved into first place among Chinese cities. More than 500 key component suppliers have grown up around the assemblers, including battery makers Gotion High-tech and CALB.

What you actually see on the trip is less about welding robots (every plant has those) and more about how quickly suppliers appear next to an assembly plant, and how a city works with an industry: funds, land, infrastructure, talent. That is the lesson you cannot download.

### Wuhu: exporting as a habit

An hour by high-speed train brings you to Wuhu, where Chery was founded in 1997. In 2025 the group sold 2.81 million vehicles, 1.34 million of them abroad. The company says it has been China’s top passenger-car exporter among domestic brands for 23 years running.

The interesting part in Wuhu is not the car itself but the export machine behind it: modular platforms, localisation for very different markets, and a river port on the Yangtze where vehicles are loaded onto ships.

Export for one year is a strategy. Export for twenty-three years is a personality.

### Shanghai: old school, new speed

Shanghai is the third stop, and the picture changes. This has been a car city for decades: SAIC, joint ventures with foreign brands, a deep layer of suppliers and engineering centres. Then there is [Tesla](/en/companies/tesla)’s Lingang plant, which shipped about 851,000 vehicles in 2025, or 52% of Tesla’s global deliveries.

Shanghai is valuable as a contrast. Hefei shows an industry built from scratch at speed. Shanghai shows an industry rebuilding itself while carrying history, habits and large joint ventures. See only Hefei, and you think anything can be done in ten months. See only Shanghai, and you think the market is already carved up.

### The bigger picture

According to the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers, China produced 34.53 million vehicles in 2025. NEV production and sales each passed 16 million, more than half of new-car sales. Exports exceeded 7 million vehicles, 2.6 million of them NEVs.

A market this size cannot afford to waste time, and it doesn’t. Hence the price wars, constant model refreshes and development timelines that make foreign engineers laugh first and go quiet second.

### Who should go

**Component makers** study what assemblers demand from suppliers, how fast samples are approved, and how line-side logistics work.

**Distributors and dealers** see the product before it reaches their showroom and learn which markets the brand truly prioritises.

**Manufacturers from other industries** come for organisation: takt time, logistics, quality control, data. A car plant is the best lean-manufacturing textbook there is, even if you make furniture.

**Industrial park developers and regional officials** will find Hefei’s story close to a manual, written in concrete.

### How the route works

Hefei, Wuhu and Shanghai are linked by high-speed rail, so the week is dense but needs no domestic flights. A typical outline: two or three days in Hefei (assembly, batteries, components, a meeting with an industrial park), a day in Wuhu, two days in Shanghai. Changzhou or Ningbo can be added for batteries or parts.

The list of companies is built around the delegation’s goals. Global Tech Tour arranges closed visits to more than 900 companies across 17 industries, but every visit is confirmed by the host. So the honest order is: your questions first, then the route, then confirmations.

### Four things not to do in a car plant

1.  Ask what the car costs “for friends”. It costs the same.
2.  Photograph the line while the guide is talking about company culture. He will notice.
3.  Compare everything out loud with a plant you saw in 1987.
4.  Try a door handle on a car moving down the line. The line won’t stop. Your visit might.

### The bottom line

In automotive China, models, prices and even city rankings age fast. A province that was eighth five years ago is now first. In another five years, someone else may be.

So don’t just look at who is ahead. Look at how they got there. You can only see that on site, preferably wearing a hard hat.
