Most city halls approach business with an inspection. Hefei approaches it with a term sheet.

That is not a figure of speech. The capital of Anhui province runs state investment platforms with assets in the hundreds of billions of yuan, teams of analysts, lawyers and auditors for due diligence, and a habit of buying into companies at the very moment nobody else wants them. Chinese commentators call it “the Hefei model”. A fair translation would be a venture fund with a coat of arms.

We have written before about the automotive route through Hefei. This piece is not about the route. It is about the mechanics: how the city makes investment decisions, and why a foreign partner should understand them before the first meeting at the local investment bureau.

### A display line that wasn’t there

In 2008 Hefei was nobody’s idea of an industrial capital, but it did have a university. The University of Science and Technology of China moved there in 1970, and for decades it was the city’s main claim to fame.

That year the city struck a deal with [BOE](/en/companies/boe) to build a sixth-generation LCD panel line. According to KrASIA, Hefei’s bid promised 17.5 billion yuan: 6 billion from the government, 3 billion from investors and the rest from local banks. The city later put in another 28 billion for two more fabs.

For a city that ranked around 80th in China by GDP at the start of the century, this was a bet on one company and one technology at once. People who bet like that are usually either very brave or very desperate. Hefei seems to have had both kinds in the room, and they agreed.

The key detail is what came next. The city did not stay a shareholder forever. It started selling after three years and booked a profit of around 700 million yuan. Today a local vehicle, Jianxiang, holds a little over 1% of BOE, with control in Beijing’s hands. The panels stayed, and so did the jobs: more than 5,600 from the sixth-generation line alone.

A subsidy is paid and forgotten. A stake is taken and, eventually, sold.

### Memory worth remembering

The second story is CXMT, a DRAM maker, launched under the code name “Project 506”. The Hefei government committed 14.4 billion yuan and its partner GigaDevice 3.6 billion. The partner, KrASIA reports, initially fell short, putting in just 300 million yuan in convertible bonds in April 2019.

Anyone who has built anything with partners knows the pattern: the promise, the pause, the creative financing instrument. In Hefei it did not kill the project, because the lead investor was already committed and could not afford to stop.

CXMT is now one of China’s leading memory makers, and the city hosts more than 300 semiconductor companies. That is what a cluster looks like: one anchor, and everyone else mooring alongside.

### NIO, or buying what everyone else was selling

Hefei’s most famous bet is [NIO](/en/companies/nio). In spring 2020 the EV maker was in serious trouble, with its shares down to around two dollars. According to CnEVPost, citing China Economic Weekly, 18 local governments had talked to NIO before the Hefei deal. One closed it.

At the end of April 2020, strategic investors from Anhui put 7 billion yuan into NIO China, the company’s core operating business, for a combined 24.1%. NIO moved its headquarters and production to Hefei. The deal was led by Hefei Urban Construction Development, a platform wholly owned by the city’s state assets commission.

The process is worth noting. The platform set up a dedicated team, tracked Beijing’s policy on new energy vehicles, hired auditors and lawyers, negotiated terms, and only then took a proposal to the city government covering value, risks and mitigation. Its chairman called this Hefei’s standard method, and said one company had chosen Hefei over a rival offering bigger incentives because Hefei “negotiated professionally and delivered strictly on what it signed”.

The numbers that followed are public. In February 2021, NIO bought back 3.305% of NIO China from two of the investors for 5.5 billion yuan. Simple arithmetic, as an illustration rather than a return calculation: the full 24.1% had cost 7 billion, and less than a seventh of it fetched nearly 80% of that sum ten months later. In September 2021 NIO bought another 1.418% for 2.5 billion.

The city has not published a final figure for its returns, and we will not guess one. The visible transactions say enough.

And the industry stayed. In 2025, according to 36Kr, Hefei became China’s top city for new energy vehicle output, with the sector worth more than 500 billion yuan.

### A stake is not a subsidy

This is the part that matters for business, and it is not about cars.

A subsidy is money with no questions attached. A stake is money with questions. The city becomes a co-owner, asks for production to be located locally, builds a supplier base around the anchor, and plans an exit, in Sinocities’ reading after around three years. The proceeds fund the next bet.

Four things follow for a foreign partner. City platforms want to be spoken to as investors: a model, numbers and clearly stated risks beat a glossy brochure. The city cares less about your office than your supply chain; its real question is who follows you. A city shareholder brings protection and obligations in equal measure. And the model has limits. Researchers point out that land sales, the main funding source for city platforms, fell 38% in 2023, and local government financing vehicles nationwide carry debts measured in trillions. Beijing has warned against “blind launching and redundant construction of projects”. A venture fund with a coat of arms can have a bad year too. It just gets fewer headlines.

### What you see on the ground

Statistics tell part of the story. Hefei’s GDP reached 1.35 trillion yuan in 2024; over two decades the city climbed from 80th place nationally into the top 20, and its population roughly doubled to over 9 million. But rankings do not explain how it was done.

Conversations do: with industrial park managers, with suppliers who followed the anchor company, with executives who have a city platform on their board. On Global Tech Tour business expeditions, those are the meetings we value most. You can watch a production line on video. Understanding why a city bet on it takes a conversation.

Some cities wait for an investor. Hefei decided to become one. Whether that took more courage or more arithmetic is an open question. Perhaps, done properly, they are the same thing.
