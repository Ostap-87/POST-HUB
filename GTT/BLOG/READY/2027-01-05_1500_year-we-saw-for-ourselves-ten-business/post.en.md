Year-in-review pieces are traditionally written in the last days of December, with a glass in one hand and an annual report in the other. We are writing this one early, which has its own kind of honesty: every number below is as of October 2026. China’s full-year statistics come out in January, and if they change the picture, we will say so.

It has been a dense year. This blog series now runs to sixty articles on Chinese cities, companies, factories, hospitals and the rules of the game. Here are the ten stories that, in our view, best explain 2026. For each we list the articles in the series that cover it in depth.

### 1. Robots stopped being a trade-show act

In the first seven months of 2026, China’s output of industrial robots rose 28.5% year on year, according to official statistics. These are not humanoids on stage but arms that weld, paint and move parts. Humanoids exist too, and more of them are leaving exhibition halls for factory floors, but most of the work is still done by robots that cannot dance.

The hardest-working robot of the year is the one nobody photographed.

Further reading: articles 08 (three robotics hubs), 15 ([Sany](/en/companies/sany)’s digital plants), 29 (telling a demo from a product at WAIC).

### 2. Car exports: more in eight months than in all of last year

According to the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers (CAAM), China exported 7.153 million vehicles in January–August 2026, up 66.7%. Of these, 3.435 million were new energy vehicles. Passenger car exports passed 6.2 million, already more than in the whole of 2025.

At home the picture is different: domestic passenger car sales fell 25.6% year on year in August. When it gets crowded at home, you head abroad. The rule holds for people and cars alike.

Further reading: articles 07 (auto expedition from Hefei to Shanghai), 24 (Chinese EVs in Indonesia and Malaysia), 27 (Auto Shanghai 2027), 31 (Hefei), 33 (Chongqing), 39 ([NIO](/en/companies/nio) battery swapping).

### 3. “Neijuan”: the word of the year that resists translation

If China picked a business word of the year, it would probably be 内卷, “involution”: competition in which everyone runs faster and nobody gets anywhere. Since mid-2025 the authorities have campaigned against endless price wars in cars, solar and delivery. Falling domestic car sales and surging exports are two sides of the same story.

Further reading: article 49 on involution, article 55 on how Singles’ Day has changed.

### 4. AI went shopping

In May 2026 [Alibaba](/en/companies/alibaba) opened the entire Taobao and Tmall catalogue, more than 4 billion products, to its Qwen AI app. The assistant picks products in conversation, stacks coupons and can place an order itself when the price drops to a target. Meanwhile the 15th Five-Year Plan launched an “AI+” programme for every industry.

A year ago AI in Chinese business was something shown at conferences. This year it is working the checkout.

Further reading: articles 29 (WAIC), 51 (OKRs and Feishu at ByteDance), 55 (11.11).

### 5. Driverless taxis became a service

Baidu’s Apollo Go completed 3.2 million fully driverless rides in the first quarter of 2026 and passed 22 million rides in total by April. Pony.ai reported positive per-vehicle economics in Guangzhou and Shenzhen. Chinese robotaxis operate in Dubai and Abu Dhabi and are testing in London, Hong Kong and Luxembourg.

Further reading: article 57.

### 6. The sky became a street

By the end of 2025 China had 3.287 million registered civil drones, and annual flight hours grew by almost 70%. Meituan’s drones had completed more than 780,000 orders in six cities. In May 2026 the civil aviation regulator set up a dedicated low-altitude safety department.

Further reading: article 56.

### 7. The world is buying Chinese medicines

In the first half of 2026, Chinese drug developers signed 81 out-licensing deals worth about 110 billion US dollars, according to National Medical Products Administration data cited by state television. That is a first-half record and already about 80% of the full-year 2025 total. A country long seen as a generics factory is now selling the rights to its own discoveries.

Further reading: articles 11 (medtech expedition), 28 (CMEF), 43–48 (healthcare in China).

### 8. Southeast Asia: “China plus one”, where the one is also a bit of China

Supply chains kept flowing into Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia, and next to almost every new plant you find a Chinese investor, Chinese equipment or a Chinese component supplier. ASEAN remains China’s largest trading partner. We have written about this a great deal because it is perhaps the most practical story for anyone working in the region.

Further reading: articles 19–24 (Vietnam, Laos, Singapore, Johor, Indonesia, Malaysia).

### 9. Money got duller and more useful

On 1 January 2026 the digital yuan stopped being digital cash and became deposit money: balances in verified wallets now earn interest. By the end of 2025 it had processed 19.5 trillion yuan, less than six per cent of what Alipay and WeChat Pay handle in a single year. Abroad, through the mBridge platform, the digital yuan has found its niche.

Further reading: article 58.

### 10. A new five-year plan: growth without a number, carbon with one

In March 2026 China adopted its 15th Five-Year Plan for 2026–2030. It has 20 headline indicators. As in the previous plan, GDP growth carries no specific figure, while a 17% cut in carbon intensity is binding. The 2026 target is growth of 4.5–5%. The economy grew 4.7% in the first half. Third-quarter data are not included here, as this review was prepared before their release.

Further reading: article 59.

### What we learned this year

If sixty articles had to be boiled down to one idea, it would be this: you cannot understand China in 2026 from averages. Average GDP growth of around five per cent hides industries growing by tens of per cent and others that are shrinking. The average shopper is cautious; the average exporter is confident. The average factory is automating; the average city is rewriting its specialisation.

That is why we call the people on our trips researchers, not tourists. Global Tech Tour runs business expeditions to China with closed visits to more than 900 companies across 17 industries. You can read the averages at home. The deviations from the average are only visible on the ground.

### The bottom line

The year we have described is not over as we put the full stop on it. There is something very Chinese about that: while you are writing up the results, the country has already taken the next step.

Thank you for reading. See you in 2027, preferably on a factory floor.
