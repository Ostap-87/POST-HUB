At an artificial intelligence show, natural intelligence is hard to hold on to. Robots dance, avatars talk, screens glow with “world first”, and your phone buzzes with a colleague asking what you have bought so far.

Buying nothing on day one is already a sign of intelligence. Possibly the highest on display.

WAIC, the World Artificial Intelligence Conference, takes place in Shanghai every summer. It is the main showcase for Chinese AI: models, chips, robots, smart devices and anything else that can be called smart with enough effort. Here is how to see it as a business, not as a tourist.

### What it is and how fast it is growing

The first WAIC was held in 2018. According to the Shanghai government, it drew 72,000 guests from more than 40 countries. Then things accelerated, as the last two editions show.

**2025, 26–28 July:** more than 800 participating companies, over 3,000 products and technologies, and a then-record 70,000 square metres of exhibition space.

**2026, 17–20 July:** more than 1,100 exhibitors, over 3,000 exhibits, more than 100,000 square metres and over 300 global AI product debuts, spread across three areas: the former Expo site, Zhangjiang Science Hall and the West Bund convention centre. Afterwards, the Pudong district government reported more than 400,000 visitors and a record 20.36 billion yuan in intended procurement, up 25% on the previous year.

The exhibition grew by almost half in a single year. The industry, it seems, is growing faster than the halls can be built.

**At the time of writing, WAIC 2027 dates have not been officially announced. Please check with the organiser.** The conference is traditionally held in Shanghai in July, but the exact week varies.

### What is on show

To simplify, WAIC has four floors.

**Models and software.** Major Chinese developers show language and multimodal models, agents and business tools. For buyers this is the hardest part to judge: a model on a stand always answers well, because the questions were prepared on the same stand.

**Compute.** Chips, servers, clusters. The engineers here are more worth talking to than the sales team.

**Robots and embodied AI.** Humanoids, dexterous hands, warehouse and service robots. The most photogenic section and therefore the trickiest. According to Global Times, the 2026 show featured a workshop where humanoids performed assembly operations on an electric vehicle line, from battery module assembly to fitting an interior speaker.

**Applications.** AI in manufacturing, healthcare, education and elderly care. In 2026 the organisers openly bet on practical use, and the WAIC Connect programme brought together 172 buyer delegations and 63 deployment “scenarios” where demand meets supply.

For business, the most useful floor is the fourth. The busiest is the third. They rarely coincide.

### The key question: demo or product?

At an AI show almost everything looks finished. That is not deception; it is the genre. A stand is a stage, not a warehouse, and stages are always well lit. Here is how to tell one from the other.

**A demo** works in ideal conditions: the lighting is set, objects sit where they should, an engineer with a laptop stands nearby. If a robot folds a towel, it is the same towel all day. If a model answers questions, a staff member asks them.

**A product** is ready for someone else’s conditions. It has a price, a lead time, warranty terms, a customer list and a person who will turn up when something breaks. You can ask a product to do something off-script, and it may do it less elegantly, but it will do it.

A simple test: ask them to repeat the demo with your object, your question or your data. The stand’s reaction will tell you more than the brochure.

### Six questions to ask at a stand

1.  Where does this work today, apart from the show? Name one customer.
2.  What does it cost, and what is the lead time?
3.  How many hours per shift does it run without human intervention?
4.  Who services it, and how fast can they get to us?
5.  What data was it trained on, and can it be fine-tuned on ours?
6.  Which of this is in series production, and which is “in the next version”?

If the answer to question six is “all of it”, ask again. Sometimes on the second try it turns out the only thing in series production is the staff T-shirt.

### Planning your visit

**Register early.** Tickets are sold through the official Hi WAIC app, and in 2026 one ticket covered all three areas. The venues are spread across the city, so plan your day by venue, not by mood.

**Pick one or two sections.** You cannot see all of WAIC in one day, and trying in two is pointless. A day on warehouse robots and a day on AI in manufacturing beats two days on everything.

**Do not skip the forums.** In 2026 there were more than 140. Half discuss the future of humanity; the other half discuss deployment at specific companies. You want the second half.

**After the conference, go to the companies.** A show reveals what companies can do. A factory reveals what that looks like at work. Shanghai and its surroundings have plenty of both AI developers and manufacturers using AI. We wrote about China’s robotics hubs in a separate article.

Global Tech Tour runs business [expeditions](/en/expeditions) to China: closed visits to companies across 17 industries, ready-made and bespoke programmes, and corporate training. The conference can be the starting point of a programme, with company visits as the follow-up. The final list of visits is always confirmed by the hosts.

### The bottom line

AI in Shanghai is presented in a way that makes you want to buy everything at once. That is a normal reaction. You just need to get through it by evening.

A robot dancing on a stand can dance. That is already something. But the one that will actually work is the one that someday steps off the stand onto someone else’s shop floor and does not ask for the lighting to be adjusted.
