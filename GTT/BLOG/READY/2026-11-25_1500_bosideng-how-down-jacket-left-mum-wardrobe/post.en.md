A down jacket is an honest product. You need it three months a year. For the other nine it hangs in a wardrobe, taking up space. Building a business on it is like selling snow: demand arrives on schedule and leaves without notice.

[Bosideng](/en/companies/bosideng) built one of China’s most profitable apparel companies on exactly that product. Twice, in fact: first as a mass brand, then as a premium one. The second time is the interesting one, because it came right after the first nearly ended.

### Eight sewing machines

The company dates itself to 1976. Founder Gao Dekang has said it started near Changshu, in Jiangsu province, with eleven tailors and eight sewing machines, doing contract work for other labels before launching its own.

Its first push into northern China in 1994 taught a lesson the company still talks about: 230,000 jackets made, about 80,000 sold, more than RMB 20 million lost on inventory. A year later it raised the down content, fixed the fit and became the category’s sales leader.

Unsold stock is the best marketing teacher there is. Expensive, but you never forget the lesson.

### The mid-2010s: a brand for your parents

By the early 2010s Bosideng was everywhere, which turned out to be the problem. It expanded into menswear, womenswear and kidswear and opened a London flagship in 2012. Meanwhile the down jacket became something you bought for your mother, not for yourself.

The year to March 2015 reads like a medical chart: revenue RMB 6.29 billion, down 23.6%; net profit RMB 132 million, down 81%. The down-apparel network shrank by a net 5,053 outlets in a single year, leaving 6,599.

Closing five thousand stores is not an optimisation. It is a verdict delivered by customers.

### 2018: a reset without a new name

Bosideng kept its name and changed nearly everything else.

In March 2018 it signed three deals at once: with state broadcaster CCTV’s national brands programme, with Wanda for prime mall locations, and with [Alibaba](/en/companies/alibaba) for online traffic. It sold or shut non-core businesses and went back to one brand and one product.

That autumn it showed at New York Fashion Week. Milan, London and a Jean Paul Gaultier collaboration followed; in 2025 came the AREAL line with British designer Kim Jones and a show in Paris, and in January 2026 an AREAL pop-up at Galeries Lafayette Haussmann.

The runway did not replace the stores; it worked alongside them. What the catwalk sold was not a jacket but permission to pay more for one.

### Before and after

Year to March 2018: revenue RMB 8.88 billion, profit attributable to shareholders RMB 615 million, gross margin 46.4%, down apparel 63.6% of sales.

Year to March 2026: revenue RMB 27.35 billion (+5.6%), profit RMB 3.99 billion (+13.7%), gross margin about 57%, down apparel 86.2% of sales.

In eight years revenue roughly tripled and profit grew about six and a half times. Measured from the 2015 trough, profit is up roughly thirtyfold, and for five years running the company has paid out more than 80% of it in dividends.

Note the share of down apparel. Bosideng did not diversify its way to growth. It grew once it stopped diversifying.

### How the price went up

Industry reporting puts Bosideng’s average down-jacket tag price above RMB 1,000. It has not reached Canada Goose territory: items above RMB 10,000 sell weakly, and the volume sits around the thousand-yuan mark.

That may be the most sober lesson here. Bosideng did not become a luxury brand. It became an expensive mass brand, and that was enough to add more than ten points of gross margin. Premium, it turns out, is not the price on the top shelf. It is the price on the middle one.

**Product:** technical lines for extreme cold, lightweight down and windbreakers. The last two tackle the category’s real enemy, seasonality: stores have something to sell in April.

**Stores:** a Shanghai flagship (2022) and a concept store in Beijing’s Sanlitun (2024). The network stays disciplined: 3,647 regular down-apparel stores at the end of March 2026, about a third self-operated, plus more than a thousand seasonal pop-ups.

**Story:** polar and Everest expeditions in the brand legend since the late 1990s, fashion weeks, collaborations. Chinese shoppers pay for the down and for the feeling of buying a world-class product from a home-grown brand.

### Five ways not to copy Bosideng

1.  Raise prices without changing the store. Customers will read the new tag against the old décor.
2.  Book a runway before fixing the warehouse.
3.  Launch ten new categories “to reduce dependence on the core”.
4.  Wait for the brand to stop being “your mum’s” on its own.
5.  Hire a famous designer for one collection and call it a strategy.

### What it teaches

Many manufacturers sit in the same trap: a good product, a known name and a reputation for “reliable, not exciting”. Bosideng shows the way out is not a new logo but a sequence: cut the excess, invest in product, then in stores, and only then in the runway.

The less pleasant lesson is time. Decisions taken in 2018 show up in the 2026 numbers. Eight years is three fashion cycles, one pandemic and countless meetings where someone suggested going back to the old price.

### Where to see it

Bosideng stores are public spaces. Any retail-focused programme in Shanghai or Beijing can include them as ordinary shoppers would: how the ranges are laid out, how price tiers are separated, how staff explain what you are paying for. ### The bottom line

Bosideng has made the same product for fifty years. The jacket of 1976 and the jacket of 2026 do the same job: they keep you warm.

The difference is who wears it and how it feels. That difference is worth roughly a thirtyfold rise in profit.

A warm product. Especially for shareholders.
