Planning a trip to a Chinese trade show a year ahead is a job for optimists. Some organisers announce dates eighteen months out, others a few months before; some shows move, change their name or switch cities. China does not like standing still, and its trade shows like it even less.

So, a fair warning up front: this calendar has two kinds of entries. Some carry dates the organisers have already announced (as of autumn 2026, when this was written). Others say “check with the organiser”. We do not guess this year’s dates from last year’s. We only tell you which season to expect.

The only calendar where everything is known in advance belongs to someone who is not going anywhere.

### Holidays first

Before choosing a show, mark what matters more in China than any exhibition. Lunar New Year falls on 6 February 2027. The State Council publishes the official holiday schedule separately, but factories usually stop for at least a week, slow down well before the holiday and take time to restart afterwards. Best not to plan a business trip for late January or February at all.

The other two turbulence zones are the May Day holidays and National Day on 1 October, known as Golden Week. Flights and hotels get expensive, and many offices close.

One more thing for Russian travellers: China has extended visa-free entry for Russian citizens to 31 December 2027, for stays of up to 30 days, including for business. Rules change, so check them before you travel.

### Spring: the busiest season

**Beijing auto show (specialised exhibition), 27 March – 5 April 2027.** Beijing and Shanghai normally take turns hosting the big comprehensive auto shows: Beijing in even years, Shanghai in odd years. In 2027 Beijing holds a separate specialised exhibition in an odd year for the first time. 27–28 March are media days, 29–30 March trade days, and the public is admitted from 31 March. Venues: the China International Exhibition Center’s new Shunyi site and the Capital International Exhibition Convention Center.

**CHINAPLAS, 13–16 April 2027, Shenzhen.** One of the world’s largest plastics and rubber shows takes place at the Shenzhen World Exhibition & Convention Center in Bao’an. It covers far more than plastic: machinery, packaging, recycling and materials for the automotive and electronics industries.

**Canton Fair, spring session: check dates with the organiser.** The fair has been held in Guangzhou every spring and autumn since 1957, in three phases. In spring 2026 the phases ran 15–19 April, 23–27 April and 1–5 May. When this article was written, the organiser had not announced official dates for spring 2027. We cover the fair in detail in a separate piece.

**CMEF, spring medical equipment fair: check dates with the organiser.** The spring CMEF is traditionally held in Shanghai; in 2026 it ran 9–12 April. It is one of the world’s largest medtech shows, attended by hospital buyers and distributors.

**Auto Shanghai, 23 April – 2 May 2027.** The 22nd Shanghai auto show will be held at the National Exhibition and Convention Center (NECC). 23–24 April are media days, 25–26 April trade days, and 27 April to 2 May public days. According to the organisers, the 2025 edition displayed 1,366 vehicles, including 163 world premieres. The 2027 show promises a dedicated zone for embodied AI and smart devices.

In spring, it is not only the blossom that comes out in China. So do the hotel prices near the exhibition halls.

### Summer: energy and artificial intelligence

**SNEC, 2–4 June 2027, Shanghai.** The 20th edition of the solar, energy storage and battery show takes place at NECC (dates per the organisers’ announcement published by Shanghai Metals Market). For anyone modelling solar and storage for their own sites, this is the event of the year.

**WAIC, World Artificial Intelligence Conference: check dates with the organiser.** Held in Shanghai in summer; in 2026 it ran 17–20 July, with more than 1,100 exhibitors and over 300 AI products making their global debut.

**World Robot Conference: check dates with the organiser.** Held in Beijing’s E-Town district; in 2026 it ran 19–23 August. If you want to see Chinese humanoids in person rather than on video, this is usually where you go.

### Autumn: trade and imports

**Canton Fair, autumn session: check dates with the organiser.** The autumn 2026 session is scheduled for 15 October to 4 November: 15–19 October, 23–27 October and 31 October to 4 November.

**CIIE, China International Import Expo: check dates with the organiser.** Held in Shanghai in November; the ninth edition in 2026 is scheduled for 5–10 November. What makes it different is that here China buys rather than sells: it is a showcase for foreign companies seeking entry into the Chinese market.

### Combining a show with factory visits

A trade show is a hundred companies in a day. A factory is one company, but for real. The best trips, in our view, combine both: at the show you see the whole market and choose whom to visit next; at the factory you check whether there is real production behind the booth.

Some combinations that work:

1.  **Shenzhen and Guangzhou in spring.** CHINAPLAS in Shenzhen, then factories in the Pearl River Delta. If the spring Canton Fair follows its usual pattern, its first phase, focused on industry and electronics, fits neatly into the same trip: the cities are neighbours linked by high-speed rail. Finalise plans only once the dates are announced.
2.  **Shanghai in late April.** Auto Shanghai plus Yangtze River Delta factories: EVs, batteries, components. Note that the last days of the show fall during the May Day holidays.
3.  **Shanghai in June.** SNEC plus battery and solar module makers in neighbouring provinces.
4.  **Beijing in late summer.** The World Robot Conference plus robotics companies in the capital’s cluster, once the dates are announced.

One rule applies to all of them: put the show at the start of the trip and the factories at the end. After the show, you know exactly what to ask.

### Five ways to waste a trade show

1.  Walk every hall in one day. Your feet will remember it; your head will not.
2.  Collect two hundred business cards and sort none of them until you get home.
3.  Skip pre-registration and spend the morning queueing for a badge.
4.  Mistake a trading company for a factory. On the stand they look the same.
5.  Fly out on closing day, leaving no time to visit the companies you found.

### The bottom line

A trade show calendar is like a train timetable: useful to have, and even more useful to check before you leave the house. In 2027 China will show new cars, new materials, new robots and, most likely, something nobody has heard of yet.

At the show you see what China wants to show you. At the factory you see what it can actually make. A good trip makes time for both.
