There are two ways to do the Canton Fair. One is to walk every hall, collect a bag of catalogues and come home convinced that China makes everything. True, but hardly news. The other is to arrive looking for three specific products, find five manufacturers and, a week later, stand on one of their factory floors.

The first is good for general knowledge. The second is good for business.

This piece is about the second. We cover Auto Shanghai, CMEF and the year’s other shows separately; here it is Guangzhou, the fair and practice only.

### What it is, and why it is so big

The China Import and Export Fair, known everywhere as the Canton Fair, has been held in Guangzhou every spring and autumn since 1957. Its 140th session, in autumn 2026, marked the fair’s 70th anniversary.

The numbers take a second reading. The 140th session covers 1.55 million square metres with more than 30,000 exhibitors. The 139th session in spring 2026 drew 314,000 overseas buyers from 220 countries and regions, displayed 4.65 million products and, by the organisers’ estimate, generated US$25.7 billion in intended export orders.

Spend one minute at each booth without sleeping, and you would need three weeks. The fair lasts three weeks. Coincidence? No, planning.

### 2027 dates

Briefly and honestly: when this article was written (autumn 2026), the organiser had not announced official dates for the spring 2027 session. Check them on the fair’s official website before buying tickets or booking a hotel.

For reference, in spring 2026 the fair ran from 15 April to 5 May, and in autumn 2026 from 15 October to 4 November, both times in three five-day phases with breaks in between. But a reference is not a schedule.

### Three phases: which one is yours

The fair is divided into three phases, each with its own product focus. Picking the wrong phase is the most expensive mistake you can make: your manufacturers will simply not be in the halls.

**Phase 1: advanced manufacturing.** Machinery and equipment, tools, electronics and appliances, lighting and electrical products, new energy vehicles and smart mobility, industrial automation, new energy. There is a dedicated service robots zone. In spring 2026, Phase 1 had around 12,000 exhibitors across 520,000 square metres, with new zones for smart wearables, displays, and consumer and agricultural drones.

**Phase 2: quality home life.** Building materials and furniture, housewares, gifts and decorations.

**Phase 3: better life.** Health and recreation, toys and products for children and mothers, fashion and textiles. There is an intelligent healthcare zone.

The section line-up is adjusted from session to session, so check the floor plan on the organiser’s site before you go. A good rule: find your category on the floor plan first, then pick your dates, not the other way round.

### Preparation: two to three months ahead

**Registration.** Overseas buyers pre-register on the fair’s official website and receive a buyer badge. Pre-registration saves time on site: a queue for badges on the first morning is the classic opening of a trip people later remember with a sigh.

**Entry.** Russian citizens can enter China visa-free until 31 December 2027 for up to 30 days, including on business. Visitors from other countries should check visa requirements for their passport. Either way, double-check the rules before travelling.

**Your list.** Draw up a short list of products, each with technical requirements, a target price, order volume and certification needs. Without that, every conversation at a booth ends with “please send us an enquiry by email”.

**App and map.** The fair has an official app with booth navigation; more than 553,000 people used it in spring 2026. Mark the booths you need in advance, not in the hall, where the network is overloaded and your legs are already gone.

**Time.** Allow at least three or four days after the fair for factory visits. The fair starts the conversation. It does not finish it.

### Telling a factory from a trading company

This is the central question of the Canton Fair, and there is no single right answer: trading companies are a normal part of the Chinese economy, and sometimes they are easier to work with. But you should know whom you are talking to. A few signs:

1.  **Range.** A factory usually makes one product group and knows everything about it. If kettles, torches and prams share a booth, you are most likely looking at a trading company.
2.  **Technical questions.** Ask about line cycle time, tolerances, materials, changeover time. An engineer answers on the spot. A trading company’s salesperson promises to “check with the factory”.
3.  **Licence.** Ask for a copy of the business licence and check whether manufacturing is listed in the business scope.
4.  **Address.** Ask where the plant is and whether you can visit. Willingness to host you on the shop floor next week says more than any catalogue.
5.  **MOQ and lead times.** A manufacturer quotes them based on its own capacity; a trading company, based on someone else’s.

The booth is a shop window. The factory floor is where the truth is kept.

### A short conversation at a booth

“Are you the manufacturer?”

“Yes, of course.”

“Where is the factory?”

“Our partner factory is in Zhejiang province.”

“And yours?”

“Ours is an office in Guangzhou. A very good office.”

(An honest answer. The question was the right one.)

### On site: a few rules

Follow your plan, not the crowd. Visit the booths on your list first, then the neighbouring rows: that is often where lesser-known but interesting manufacturers are.

Photograph each sample together with the business card, in one shot. You will thank yourself in the evening, because two hundred unlabelled photos of kettles are impossible to tell apart.

Sign nothing at the booth. A price quoted at the fair is where negotiations begin, not where they end.

Hold a short debrief every evening: who stood out, whom to visit, what questions remain. The fair is exhausting, and after three days all booths start to look the same.

### After the fair: the factory visit

The most valuable part starts when the halls close. The manufacturers you shortlisted are mostly in Guangdong, Zhejiang, Fujian and Jiangsu, often several hours away. A factory visit shows what a booth cannot: equipment, order on the floor, quality control, people.

Global Tech Tour runs business [expeditions](/en/expeditions) to China and closed visits to companies across 17 industries, with ready-made and bespoke programmes. The Canton Fair can be the starting point of such a programme, with factory visits as its continuation. The final list of visits is always confirmed by the hosts.

### The bottom line

The Canton Fair is less an exhibition than a city that appears twice a year and disappears three weeks later. You can get lost in it, or you can find a supplier for the next ten years.

The difference between those outcomes starts not in Guangzhou but at home, the moment you decide exactly what you are going for.

Thirty thousand booths. You need one. Start looking before you fly.
