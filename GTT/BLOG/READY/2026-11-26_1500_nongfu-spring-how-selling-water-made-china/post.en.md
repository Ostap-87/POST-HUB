Some products are hard to differentiate. Water is the champion. You cannot make it tastier without it ceasing to be water. You cannot add features. It has exactly one job, and a boiled puddle can do that job too.

On this product Zhong Shanshan built a company that earned RMB 15.9 billion in net profit in 2025. In October 2025 the Hurun list named him China’s richest person again, worth about RMB 530 billion.

How do you sell what comes out of the tap? It turns out the question is not what you sell, but where, to whom, and from how far away.

### Thirty years, two slogans

Nongfu Spring was founded in Hangzhou in 1996. Its first famous line was “a little bit sweet”. Water that is a little sweet is no longer water; it is a promise. A later slogan was sharper still: “We don’t make water. We are only nature’s porter.”

Look at the structure. The company declines credit for the product and claims credit for the delivery. Rare modesty, and very profitable.

### The source is the brand

Nongfu Spring sells natural water from its own sources and says so in every ad. The company now has 16 major water sources across China, three of them added in 2025. In March 2026 it opened a plant at Jiaozi Snow Mountain in Yunnan: a source at 2,600 metres, a 41 km pipeline and more than RMB 1 billion invested.

Why so many sources? Because water is heavy and cheap. Trucking it across China makes no sense, and the company says each plant serves an average radius of about 500 km.

The logic is almost military: the product does not travel to the customer; the source moves closer to them. Geography is not the backdrop for this business. Geography is the business.

### Distribution: 2.37 million counters

The most detailed sales data came in the 2020 IPO prospectus. At the end of 2019 the company had 4,280 distributors and more than 2.37 million retail points, about 79% of them in third-tier cities and below. Over 360,000 stores had branded Nongfu Spring fridges, and nearly 60,000 vending machines operated in around 300 cities.

That is the real business model. A bottle costs a couple of yuan, and nobody crosses town for it. People buy water where they get thirsty, so the winner is not the brand with the best water but the one whose fridge is closest to the thirst.

E-commerce, incidentally, brings in only about 5% of revenue. In the age of marketplaces, one of China’s richest men made his fortune at the corner shop.

### 2024: a hit to the core product

In late February 2024 a wave of online attacks targeted the company and its founder, covering everything from label design to family history. The company called it malicious defamation and said it badly hurt sales.

The numbers agree: packaged water revenue fell 21.3% in 2024. The response was unexpected. Nongfu Spring launched purified water in a green bottle and began competing in the cheap segment it had previously ignored.

In the same year, tea grew fast enough to make up for almost all of it.

### The second engine: tea without sugar

Nongfu Spring launched Oriental Leaf, an unsweetened bottled tea, in 2011, when Chinese consumers were used to sweet drinks and the idea looked odd. According to iiMedia, China’s unsweetened drinks market grew from RMB 2.26 billion in 2015 to RMB 40.2 billion in 2023.

The company arrived years before the market did. Patience is a product too; it just doesn’t come in bottles.

### The 2025 numbers

Revenue RMB 52.55 billion, up 22.5% and above 50 billion for the first time. Net profit RMB 15.87 billion, up 30.9%. Gross margin 60.5%, up from 58.1%.

Tea became the largest category at RMB 21.6 billion, 41.1% of revenue, up 29%. Water returned to growth at RMB 18.7 billion, up 17.3%. Functional drinks added RMB 5.76 billion, juice RMB 5.18 billion.

That is a net margin of about 30%. Plenty of tech companies would love that margin on products that took years to develop. Here the product is water and tea leaves.

### A short glossary for positioning a simple product

1.  “A little bit sweet”: about a taste that isn’t there.
2.  “We are only the porter”: about credit politely declined.
3.  “2,600 metres”: a number nobody will check and everybody will remember.
4.  “Near you”: about the fridge by the till.
5.  “Zero sugar”: about what is missing, which customers pay extra for.

### What travels

For FMCG manufacturers, the Nongfu Spring lesson has three parts.

First, in a cheap product the brand is built on origin. A source, a farm, a region, a place on the map persuades better than adjectives.

Second, logistics is strategy, not cost. A network of plants tied to delivery radius is hard for a rival to copy in a year.

Third, launch the second product before the first stops growing. The company needed tea in 2024; it launched it in 2011.

### Where to see it

The most revealing part of Nongfu Spring is not the plant but the shop around the corner: the fridge, the shelf, the price tag, the tea range next to the water. ### The bottom line

Water is the simplest product there is, which makes it easy to see what a company really sells: trust in a place, closeness to the customer and a willingness to wait.

Zhong Shanshan did not invent water. He worked out how to get it to every counter in the country.

The rest is just a little bit sweet.
